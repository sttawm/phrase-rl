===RULES===
1. Rewrite the instruction as a short, plain imperative that keeps the same objects and goal.
2. Output only the rewritten instruction itself, as one line of plain text — no labels or headers (never "Thinking Process:"), no quotes around it, no reasoning, no commentary before or after.
3. If the instruction is already twelve words or fewer and contains no politeness, filler, or roundabout description, copy it out exactly unchanged — even if it is telegraphic or oddly worded ("take bowl off plate", "upright basil bottle cardboard fence" are correct as they stand; do not fix their grammar).
4. Start the rewrite with the action verb. Delete politeness, requests, addressee talk, and framing clauses entirely: "please", "kindly", "could you", "I would appreciate", "you must", "assuming/provided that …", "in order to …", "proceed to …", "notwithstanding …".
5. Keep the instruction's own verb-and-preposition frame exactly ("pick up X", "take X off Y", "put X in/on Y", "fold X from A to B"); never restate the action as its outcome ("take the bowl off the plate", not "move the bowl so it is no longer on the plate"). Only when the action itself is described in a roundabout way ("increase its altitude", "hoist it clear of the surface") translate it to the plain verb it means: pick up, put, take, move, fold, unfold, open, close.
6. Never add detail the instruction does not state: no colours, sizes, or materials, no "toy", and no objects or places it does not mention. Never trade a stated noun for a different word — "sweet potato" stays "sweet potato", "cloth" stays "cloth", "pan" stays "pan", "clothes" stays "clothes".
7. When an object is named only by a description, replace the description with the single everyday kitchen word it means: "the implement used for lifting food" → "the spatula", "the transparent vessel used for drinking" → "the glass cup", "the woven fabric used for wiping" → "the cloth". Prefer ordinary nouns (pot, pan, lid, cup, bowl, spatula, fork, cloth, towel, drawer, fridge, sink, burner, cutting board); never "vessel", "receptacle", "container", "implement", "fabric".
8. Keep every stated spatial detail in plain form (left/right, top/bottom, corner, edge, "from A to B", in/on/under/off) and keep the whole rewrite to about ten words; shorten by cutting filler words, never by dropping the location or the goal.
===RATIONALE===
What I kept: rule 1 word for word. It is the measured anchor (book delta −0.399 on the last eval), and the paired outcomes show its core intent is right where its precondition holds — on adversarial bases the book gained +1.54 all-in and +1.92 when a real rewrite was actually produced (n=70). Everything added below repairs the two ways that book lost ground, each tied to a number in rewrite_outcomes.csv or the evidence bank.

1. Rule 2 (output contract) attacks the single largest measured loss: 79 of 365 rewrites (22%) came out as the literal string "Thinking Process:", averaging −2.944 delta; real rewrites averaged +0.207. Mechanically, eliminating the leakage moves the whole-book mean from ≈−0.48 to ≈+0.21 with no other change. This was flagged by the iter_00 audit and is still happening in every regime (37 natural, 26 search, 10 adversarial, 6 original rows).

2. Rule 3 (verbatim guard) resolves the regime split. Real rewrites of already-clean inputs lose: natural −0.287, original −0.508, search −0.394, while the canonical original phrasing sits at or near the top of its task in 99% of tasks (mean within-task score 0.991, median 1.000, n=156). I tested the guard's exact condition against the paired outcomes: it fires on 129 clean-base rows currently averaging about −0.15 to −0.44, and on zero adversarial rows — so it converts a measured loss to an exact 0.0 without touching the +1.92 repair regime. The twelve-word threshold comes from the corpus (mean ≈10 words, bulk 6–14) and from the monotone length gradient in the bank (mean score 0.892 at 1–4 words falling to 0.319 at 21+). The unmeasured claim: verbatim copy beats the −0.15 average on guarded rows. Test: measure verbatim returns on short plain natural/search bases as a probe. Known cost accepted: the guard also protects rare short-but-wrong-frame phrasings ("Move the bowl so it is no longer sitting on the plate", task-worst at score 0.00) that a frame-restoring rewrite might fix; measure that as a second probe ("take the bowl off the plate" vs that base) before narrowing the guard.

3. Rules 6 and 7 replace the old reading of "keeps the same objects", which the applier interpreted as license to re-ground objects against the scene. Measured: rewrites of natural/original bases that added colour words averaged −0.738 vs −0.082 without (n=48/85); adding "toy" averaged −0.430 (n=23); the worst single loss (−5.93) stuffed three colours into a clean instruction; noun swaps lost consistently (sweet potato→"yellow toy potato" −3.45, pan→pot −2.39, clothes→fabric −1.09, lid→"metallic cover" −0.69, basil bottle→"red spice container" −1.99). The opposite translation — roundabout description to plain noun — is where the adversarial wins came from (+4.71, +3.14 on the spatula pairs), and the bank agrees: top-scoring phrases are 28% outside the corpus's common vocabulary vs 54% for bottom ones. Rule 7's noun list is the corpus's own head terms. Conflict resolved explicitly: colour is corpus-typical (≈0.64 colour words per training instruction), but ADDING colour the base didn't state measured negative, so the ban stands; whether correcting a wrong stated colour helps is genuinely unsettled (the two orange→yellow cloth swaps scored −3.07 and +0.09) — worth a controlled probe pair before any rule licenses it.

4. Rule 5 (keep the verb frame) encodes the strongest within-task pattern in the bank: phrasings that keep the canonical verb+preposition frame score 0.95+ ("Get the bowl off the plate" 1.00, "Place the knife onto the cutting board" 0.96), while outcome-paraphrases crater even when short and plain ("Move the bowl so it is no longer sitting on the plate" 0.00; "Move your gripper from the first object to the second" 0.04 on a task whose jargon original scores 1.00). Its second sentence gives the translation path for ornate action descriptions, which is the only case where changing the verb is licensed — the last remaining adversarial losses (−2.57 "Grip the clear glass and lift it off the rack", which also invented "the rack") came from free-styling the verb and landmark instead of translating to "pick up the glass cup" (that task's original, score 1.00).

5. Rules 4 and 8 pin down "plain" and "short" with the corpus's own register: politeness/addressee markers appear in 43% of bottom-quintile phrases vs 9% of top; the corpus contains no please/kindly/you/could at all; and spatial words are its largest descriptive system (≈1.4 per instruction), so brevity must never cut them — the fold/unfold family keeps its "from A to B" tail in every top phrasing.
