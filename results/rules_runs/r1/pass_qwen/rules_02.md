===RULES===
1. Rewrite the instruction as a short, plain imperative that keeps the same objects and goal.
2. Output only the rewritten instruction itself, as one line of plain text — no labels or headers (never "Thinking Process:"), no quotes around it, no reasoning, no commentary before or after.
3. First check whether the instruction needs rewriting at all. If it is twelve words or fewer and contains no politeness, no addressee talk, and no roundabout description, output it exactly as it stands — character for character, keeping its capitalisation, punctuation, articles, stated colours, and small words ("up", "over", "leftward") unchanged, even if it is telegraphic or ungrammatical ("take bowl off plate", "upright basil bottle cardboard fence" are correct as they are; do not fix their grammar). Copying is the normal case and rewriting the exception. The only edit permitted on such an instruction is completing a truncated direction into the full "from A to B" form ("from top down" → "from top to bottom").
4. Start the rewrite with the action verb. Delete politeness, requests, addressee talk, and framing clauses entirely: "please", "kindly", "could you", "I would appreciate", "you must", "assuming/provided that …", "in order to …", "proceed to …", "notwithstanding …".
5. Keep the instruction's own verb-and-preposition frame exactly ("pick up X", "take X off Y", "put X in/on Y", "fold X from A to B"); never restate the action as its outcome ("take the bowl off the plate", not "move the bowl so it is no longer on the plate"). Only when the action itself is described in a roundabout way ("increase its altitude", "hoist it clear of the surface") translate it to the plain verb it means: pick up, put, take, move, fold, unfold, open, close.
6. Never add detail the instruction does not state: no colours, sizes, or materials, no "toy", and no objects or places it does not mention — this holds during full rewrites of ornate instructions too ("put the spatula on the plate", not "on the pink plate"). Never trade a stated noun for a different word: "sweet potato" stays "sweet potato", "pan" stays "pan", and vague nouns are real nouns to keep — "the red object" stays "the red object", never "the red spatula"; object, thing, item, figure are words to preserve, not descriptions to resolve.
7. When an object is named only by a description, replace the description with the single everyday kitchen word it means: "the implement used for lifting food" → "the spatula", "the transparent vessel used for drinking" → "the glass cup", "the woven fabric used for wiping" → "the cloth". Prefer ordinary nouns (pot, pan, lid, cup, bowl, spatula, fork, cloth, towel, drawer, fridge, sink, burner, cutting board); never "vessel", "receptacle", "container", "implement", "fabric".
8. Keep every stated spatial detail in plain form (left/right, top/bottom, corner, edge, "from A to B", in/on/under/off), including where the object starts ("out of the pot", "from the basket") and both halves of an "or" choice ("the pot or the pan"). Keep the whole rewrite to about ten words; shorten by cutting filler words only, never the location, the source, or the goal.
