===RULES===
1. Output exactly one line: the rewritten instruction itself, and nothing else — no headings, labels, bullet points, quotation marks, reasoning, or notes of any kind (never text like "Thinking Process:"). If unsure what to write, copy the input instruction unchanged rather than output anything else.
2. If the instruction is already a short direct command — it starts with a base-form verb (like "put", "take", "fold"; not "moved", "puts", or "putting") or is a terse caption fragment (like "put pan in sink", "lever vertical to front"), runs under about 15 words, and contains no "please", "kindly", "I", "me", or "you", and no multi-word description standing in for an object's name — output it as given and stop; no later rule applies to it. You may make exactly one kind of change to such an instruction: replace a formal or unusual name for a thing or place with the everyday kitchen word ("washbasin" or "basin" → "sink", "textile" or "fabric" → "cloth"). Otherwise do not add or remove articles, fix spelling or grammar, change capitalisation or punctuation, or swap any word for a synonym.
3. Rewrite the instruction as a short, plain imperative that keeps the same objects and goal.
4. When a multi-word description stands in for an object's name, replace it with the everyday one-word name it identifies ("the handheld implement for lifting food" → "the spatula"); if the description fits several objects, pick the most ordinary kitchen word that fits ("the handled cooking vessel" → "the pot"), never keep the ornate description. But when the instruction already names something in one or two plain words — even vaguely, like "object", "thing", or "robot hand" — keep those exact words and substitute nothing. If you cannot tell what object is meant at all, copy the whole instruction unchanged.
5. Keep every stated detail of the objects and places — colour, size, number, material, position, and both sides of an "or" choice ("pot or pan" stays "pot or pan", never just "pot") — and add none: never introduce a colour, size, number, or location the instruction does not state. Wrappers are not details: drop politeness, conditions ("if…", "provided that…", "assuming…"), and any leftover words of a description you have already replaced.
6. Keep the small words of any part that is already plain — "lift the pot up out of the sink" keeps "up", "take the lid off" keeps "off", and directions stay in the form "from A to B". When replacing an ornate verb, use a common one: put, take, pick up, place, move, open, close, fold, unfold. When a rewrite takes an object out of or off one place and puts it in or on another, write two linked commands ("pick up the spatula and put it on the plate"), not "move X from A to B" — but never restructure an instruction that rule 2 already passed through.
7. When the instruction is about moving the robot's own hand, gripper, or arm itself — nothing being picked up, carried, or placed — write it with the words "end effector" (e.g. "end effector transition from the first piece to the second piece"), reusing the instruction's own names for the start and end points; never write "hand", "gripper", or "arm" in its place, and never translate an instruction that already says "end effector" into everyday words.
