===RULE NOTES===
1. **Adherence:** perfect in the sample — all 40 outputs are a single bare instruction line; the "Thinking Process:" failure mode that poisoned 79 of 226 rewrites in the last measurement round (mean delta -2.94) does not appear once. **Performance:** unmeasurable in isolation from these samples precisely because it never fails here, but the book's delta jumped from +0.170 to +0.408 while the rewrite logic largely carried over, and eliminating the leak is the most parsimonious explanation for a large share of that jump. **What to do:** keep it word for word, and keep it first. It has no observed downside; its copy-the-input fallback is what makes rule 2's no-ops exactly zero-cost. Nothing to change; the only evidence that would justify touching it is a reappearance of meta-text in outputs, which would mean the wording isn't strong enough for the applier and should become even more mechanical ("your entire response must parse as one imperative sentence").

2. **Adherence:** high but not perfect. 25 of 40 bases were passed through character-exact (every one shows delta exactly +0.000, so pass-through is genuinely free), including the traps that hurt last iteration: 'Lift the pot up out of the washbasin.' (rewriting it cost -1.50 last time), 'wipe pot with sponge' (-0.21), 'move the orange cup on the green plate' (the green→yellow fabrication, -0.47), 'place the orange carrot inside the empty pot' (-0.99). Two bases that met every stated condition were rewritten anyway: 'put fork from basket to tray' (terse caption, rewritten by rule 6's transfer clause into 'pick up fork from basket and put it in tray', -0.384 — on a base that is the task string verbatim) and 'Transfer the robot hand from the first piece to the second one.' (verb-initial, 11 words, rewritten at -0.732). Both overrides lost; rule 2 was right both times. **Performance when obeyed:** on the same 25 bases the old book netted -1.156 (it "fixed" articles, spelling, and colours); this book nets exactly 0.000 — about +0.046 per sampled instruction recovered, on top of removing all fabrication risk. The forgone wins the pass-through gives up ('Grab the sweet potato...' would have gained +0.894 rewritten last iter; shut→close +0.342; 'flatten...top down' +0.335) are real but smaller than the prevented losses, confirming the trade. **What to do:** keep the conditions as they are and fix the precedence, which is the actual failure: append one sentence — "This rule outranks every rule below it: if it applies, output the copy and stop." The fork loss is not a rule-2 wording problem but a rule-6 scoping problem (see 6); fixing it in both places is cheap and unambiguous.

3. **Adherence:** good on 13 of 15 rewrites — short, verb-first, plain. Two lapses, both traceable to rule 5 rather than to this rule's wording: 'If the small box is entirely unobstructed, pick up the bowl and put it into the small box.' (18 words, comma, a conditional opener — not a plain imperative) and 'Put the orange, cone-shaped carrot on the cutting board.' (riddle fragments and a comma retained). **Performance:** this is the third book in a row carrying this rule verbatim and its regime is now clean: the 15 rewritten bases average +1.748 here versus +0.817 for the same 15 bases under the old one-rule book, with only two negatives, both adherence failures. Now that rule 2 confines it to verbose/indirect inputs, it operates exactly where the whole-book evidence says rewriting pays (+1.65 mean on adversarial bases in the outcomes data). **What to do:** keep word for word. Its two in-sample lapses are fixed by re-scoping rule 5, not by touching this.

4. **Adherence:** strong, and visibly better than last iteration. Both spatula circumlocutions were decoded to "spatula" this time (+5.789 and +5.598; last iteration one was left as "handheld implement designed for lifting food" and managed only +1.732 on the same base — the closest thing we have to a controlled per-clause measurement, worth ~+4 logits). "Hemispherical dish intended for liquids" → "bowl" (+1.727, and without the invented "blue" of last iteration). The keep-vague clause was honored where it matters most: "the red object" was kept as-is and the rewrite landed on the canonical task wording for +4.964 — last round's outcomes file shows the same base guessed into "red spatula" at -1.21, so this clause alone swung ~+6 on that base. One violation: "robot hand" → "gripper" (-0.732), substituting a word the training corpus never contains for a name that was already fine. One instructive boundary case: "handled cooking vessel" fits both pot and pan; the rule as written says keep the instruction's own word when the description is ambiguous, but the instruction's own word IS the ornate description, and keeping it lost -0.408 last iteration while this iteration's disobedient guess of "pot" won +1.710 (even though the task says pan). **Performance:** the engine of the book — the four largest deltas in the sample all run through this rule. **What to do:** amend the escape clause to distinguish the two ambiguity cases: if the instruction already names the object in one or two plain words (object, thing, robot hand), keep those words exactly and substitute nothing; if an ornate description fits several objects, replace it with the most ordinary single word it fits (pot, pan, bowl) rather than keeping the description. That legalizes the pot guess that won, keeps forbidding the gripper swap that lost, and leaves the red-object behaviour untouched.

5. **Adherence:** followed — too literally. The wins it was written for materialized: zero invented colours or locations this round (versus three fabrications last round), and 'Pick up the bowl.' shows the applier now resists decorating. But "keep every stated detail exactly ... never drop one" was read as covering things that are not object attributes: the conditional preamble "Provided that the small box is entirely unobstructed" survived as "If the small box is entirely unobstructed, ..." — and that rewrite scored 6.211 where last iteration's preamble-free 'Put the bowl into the small box.' scored 7.200 on the same base, a ~1.0 logit cost paid to over-obedience. Likewise "orange, cone-shaped" rode along on the decoded carrot (+1.024 — positive, but the phrase kept a riddle fragment and a comma, and plain carrot phrasings in the evidence bank sit visibly higher). **Performance:** where it did what it was meant to do (block fabrication, protect or-choices) it removed every loss of that class from the sample; where it over-reached it measurably capped two of the rewrites. This is high adherence with a scoping bug, not a bad idea. **What to do:** define "detail" and add an exclusion, e.g.: "Details are properties of objects and places — colour, size, number, material, position, and both sides of an 'or' choice. Wrappers are not details: drop conditions ('if/provided/assuming ...'), politeness, and any leftover words of a description already replaced under rule 4." Under that wording the bowl rewrite loses its conditional and the carrot becomes 'Put the orange carrot on the cutting board.' (orange is a stated colour and stays; cone-shaped goes).

6. **Adherence:** mixed, and both directions are informative. Obeyed where intended: the two-linked-commands form produced the sample's top rewrites ('Pick up the spatula and put it on the plate in the sink.' +5.598, 'Take the red object out of the pot and put it on the left burner.' +4.964), and small-word preservation held ('Lift the pot up ...' passed through intact; "from A to B" retained everywhere it appeared). Misfired once: the transfer clause fired on 'put fork from basket to tray', a base rule 2 had already qualified, and converted the task-verbatim string into an 11-word paraphrase at -0.384 — the clearest rules-pulling-against-each-other finding in this book, and rule 6 won the collision when rule 2 should have. Violated once: 'Move the gripper from the first piece to the second one.' uses the discouraged Move-from-to frame (-0.732), though a hand transition is arguably outside "takes an object out of one place" — borderline as adherence, still a loss. **Performance:** inside rule-3 rewrites this rule is strongly positive (it shaped the two biggest wins); as an override of rule 2 it is negative every time it has been observed. **What to do:** scope the transfer clause explicitly: begin it "When you are already rewriting under rule 3, ..." or end it "never apply this to an instruction rule 2 passed through unchanged." Keep the small-words and common-verb clauses verbatim — they are doing their job, and the verb list's exclusion of non-corpus words like "gripper" is worth making explicit if rule 4's amendment doesn't already catch it.

**Book-level:** +0.408 versus +0.170 for the previous best — the revision more than doubled the measured delta. On the identical 40-base sample the mean moved from +0.278 to +0.656; every remaining negative delta (2 of 40, worst -0.732) is an adherence failure rather than an obeyed-rule-gone-wrong, which is the healthiest failure profile a rulebook can show: the next gains are precedence/scoping fixes (2↔6, 5↔3/4), not new ideas.

===SUGGESTIONS===
Measure 'Put the carrot on the cutting board.' vs 'Put the orange carrot on the cutting board.' vs 'Put the orange, cone-shaped carrot on the cutting board.' on the carrot task — quantifies exactly what keeping riddle fragments and the colour each cost, calibrating rule 5's new "detail" boundary.
Measure 'Put the bowl into the small box.' vs 'If the small box is entirely unobstructed, pick up the bowl and put it into the small box.' as a same-draw pair — the cross-iteration gap was ~1.0 logit from different draws; a paired run confirms the cost of retained conditionals before rule 5 is re-scoped around it.
Measure 'Pick up the pot and put it in the sink.' vs 'Pick up the pan and put it in the sink.' vs 'Put the handled cooking vessel in the dish-washing basin.' on the put-pan-in-sink task — tells us whether a wrong-but-ordinary container guess really beats both the right guess's margin and the kept circumlocution, which decides how bold rule 4's ambiguity clause should be.
Measure 'Move the robot hand from the first piece to the second one.' vs 'Move the gripper ...' vs 'Move the hand ...' on the end-effector task — separates the non-corpus-word penalty (gripper) from that task's generally low ceiling.
Measure 'put fork from basket to tray' vs 'pick up fork from basket and put it in tray' as a same-draw pair — one draw showed -0.384 for the expansion; a paired confirmation locks in the rule-6-must-not-override-rule-2 fix with direct evidence.
Measure 'Put the spatula on the plate in the sink.' vs 'Put the spatula on top of the plate at the sink.' — the two winning spatula rewrites differed only in preposition scaffolding (7.259 vs 7.454 from different bases); a paired test would say whether "on top of ... at the" beats the corpus-typical "on ... in the", which would refine rule 6's guidance on prepositions.
