===RULES===
1. Output exactly one line: the rewritten instruction itself, and nothing else — no headings, labels, bullet points, quotation marks, reasoning, or notes of any kind (never text like "Thinking Process:"). If unsure what to write, copy the input instruction unchanged rather than output anything else.
2. If the instruction is already a short direct command — it starts with a base-form verb (like "put", "take", "fold"; not "moved", "puts", or "putting") or is a terse caption fragment (like "put pan in sink", "lever vertical to front"), runs under about 15 words, and contains no "please", "kindly", "I", "me", or "you", and no multi-word description standing in for an object's name — output it exactly as given: do not add or remove articles, fix spelling or grammar, change capitalisation or punctuation, or swap any word for a synonym.
3. Rewrite the instruction as a short, plain imperative that keeps the same objects and goal.
4. When a multi-word description stands in for an object's name, replace it with the everyday one-word name it unmistakably identifies ("the handheld implement for lifting food" → "the spatula"); if the description could fit more than one object, or the instruction already names it with a single word — even a vague one like "object" or "thing" — keep the instruction's own word and never substitute a guess.
5. Keep every stated detail exactly and add none: never introduce a colour, size, number, or location the instruction does not state; never drop one it does state; and keep both sides of an "or" choice ("pot or pan" stays "pot or pan", never just "pot").
6. Keep the small words of any part that is already plain — "lift the pot up out of the sink" keeps "up", "take the lid off" keeps "off", and directions stay in the form "from A to B". When replacing an ornate verb, use a common one: put, take, pick up, place, move, open, close, fold, unfold. For an instruction that takes an object out of or off one place and puts it in or on another, write two linked commands ("pick up the spatula and put it on the plate"), not "move X from A to B".

===RATIONALE===
What the new evidence showed, and what each change answers:

1. **The dominant failure was output leakage, not wording.** 79 of 226 measured rewrites (35%) were the literal string "Thinking Process:" — the applier's reasoning header emitted in place of the instruction — at a mean delta of -2.94 (up to -5.59 on canonical bases). Including leaks the book measured -0.91 per rewrite; excluding them, +0.18. Rule 1 exists solely to close this hole, with a copy-the-input fallback because a verbatim copy costs ~-0.08 while a leak costs ~-3. This is the highest-value rule in the book and it costs nothing when the applier is behaving.

2. **The training-canonical wording is the ceiling; rewriting near-canonical inputs only loses.** The original instruction is the best measured phrase in 124/156 tasks (mean within-task score 0.992); where beaten, the median gap is 0.06 logits — noise. Regime split of clean (non-leaked) rewrites: adversarial bases +1.646 mean (24/29 wins); natural bases -0.123 (median -0.004); original bases -0.257 with 1 win in 10 (the book added colours, dropped "or pan", inserted articles); search bases -0.285 changed vs -0.075 no-op. Leaving natural/search/original bases untouched is worth roughly +0.12 to +0.26 per rewrite over what the book actually did. Rule 2 encodes that: pass through anything already in house style, including misspellings and article-less telegraphese ("washing machine" for "laundry machine" cost -0.18; article insertion on "put fork from basket to tray" cost -0.97). The <15-word threshold matches the score cliff in the evidence (mean score 0.71–0.81 up to 15 words, collapsing to 0.32 beyond 21).

3. **Rule 3 is the old book's single rule, kept word for word.** Its measurements carry over, and they are good precisely where rule 2 now confines it: the +1.65-per-rewrite adversarial regime (wins of +5.60, +5.39, +4.93, +3.28, +3.21). Verb-initial phrases outscore non-verb-initial 0.735 vs 0.568; past-tense/gerund/third-person openers score 0.31–0.39, so converting those modes to imperative stays in scope.

4. **Rules 4–6 are the auditor's iter_00 findings, now confirmed at scale, written as guards.** Decoding riddle descriptions to everyday names is where the huge wins live (same-task contrast: kept circumlocution 2.91 logit vs decoded 7.26), but guessing beyond the description is the mirror loss: "red object" → guessed "red spatula" -1.21, garbled base → guessed "clear glass" -2.57; "object/thing" are corpus-normal words (≈2,300 tokens) and must survive (rule 4). Invented or dropped details lost repeatedly: added colour + dropped "or pan" -1.03, invented "pink cup … in the sink" -1.87, "or the pan" collapsed -1.86/-1.63, green→yellow -0.47 (rule 5). Dropped particles and reframed transfers lost: "lift the pot up" minus "up" -1.27/-2.06, "in half up"→"upward" -1.50, direction restructure -2.45; "move X from A to B" phrases average score 0.511 vs 0.771 for "pick up X and put it" (rule 6, with the common-verb list drawn from the corpus's top verb families).

Conflicts resolved: the auditor's iter_00 worry that ultra-short phrases score badly was a leak artifact — with "Thinking Process:" rows removed, 1–3-word phrases average 0.904, so "short" in rule 3 stands. The auditor's "leave already-plain inputs alone" hypothesis, then untested, is now directly supported by the regime table and becomes rule 2. Politeness frames per se ("Can you…") measure near-neutral at matched length (0.772 vs 0.736), so no dedicated politeness rule; rules 2/3 handle wrappers implicitly.

Claims not yet measured, which the next iteration should test as probes: (a) rule 2's pass-through applied at book level — measure verbatim-copied bases as rewrites on ~6 original/natural/search inputs to confirm the ~+0.1–0.25 no-op gain survives in situ; (b) paired "pick up the X and put it in the Y" vs "move the X from the A to the B" on five transfer tasks (put pan from stove to sink, put fork from basket to tray, put cup from sink to drying rack, take spatula off plate sink, take the red object out of the pot…); (c) paired ± invented colour ("put the pepper in the pot or pan" vs "put the red pepper…"); (d) paired keep-vs-drop "or pan" on the pot-or-pan family; (e) paired particle keep/drop ("take the lid off the pot" vs "take the lid of the pot", "lift the pot up out of the washbasin" vs "lift the pot out…"); (f) paired past-narration vs imperative ("moved the silver pot to the left side of the table" vs "move the silver pot…") to confirm the imperative-conversion clause of rule 3 on the corpus's ~10% narration mode.
