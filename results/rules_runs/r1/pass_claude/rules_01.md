===RULES===
1. Output exactly one line containing only the final instruction — no headings, labels, reasoning, quotes, or any other text. Never output the words "Thinking Process".
2. First decide whether to rewrite at all. If the instruction already starts with an action verb, is 14 words or fewer, addresses nobody (no "please", "kindly", "could you", "I need you", "for me"), and names every object with a common everyday word, copy it out exactly, character for character — do not fix capitalisation, articles, spelling, or punctuation — and ignore the remaining rules.
3. Rewrite the instruction as a short, plain imperative that keeps the same objects and goal.
4. Change as little as possible: delete politeness, requests, and framing ("please", "could you", "I would be grateful", "I need you to", "make sure", "for me"), start with the action verb, state the action instead of the resulting state ("make sure the box is closed" → "close the box"; "move the bowl so it is no longer on the plate" → "take the bowl off the plate"), and keep the rest of the original wording word for word wherever you can.
5. When an object is described rather than named, or named with an uncommon word, call it by its plain everyday name: "the hemispherical dish intended for liquids" → "the bowl", "the miniature parcel" → "the small box", "carton" → "box", "washbasin" → "sink". Everyday names are single common nouns like pot, pan, cloth, towel, spoon, bowl, cup, plate, drawer, sink, stove, burner, box, lid.
6. Keep every object, colour, source, destination, landmark, and either/or choice the instruction states ("in the pot or pan" stays "in the pot or pan"; "from the basket" stays; "in the cardboard fence" stays). Never add a colour, size, material, location, or object the instruction does not state.
7. Use plain manipulation verbs: move, put, place, take, pick up, open, close, fold, unfold, turn, push, pull, lift, flip, remove, set, grab. Replace ornate verbs ("transfer", "relocate", "deposit", "convey", "hoist", "grip", "reorient", "elevate") with the closest one from that list, and never replace a verb that is already plain.

===RATIONALE===
What the new evidence showed, in order of importance:

1. **Output leakage is the single biggest loss.** 79 of 226 rewrites (35%) came out as the literal string "Thinking Process:" instead of an instruction, at a mean delta of −2.94; real rewrites averaged +0.18. The leak is uniform across input regimes, so it is applier output discipline, not input-driven. Rule 1 attacks it directly. This is an unmeasured claim: the test is simply the next run's rephrased rows — the leak strings are in the bank now, and their disappearance (and the book mean on every regime) is the measurement.

2. **Already-plain inputs should never be touched.** In rewrite_outcomes, real rewrites of `original` bases went 0 positive / 10 non-positive (mean −0.26); `search` bases averaged −0.22 (9 pos / 39 zero-or-neg); `natural` bases netted −0.12. In the full bank, `original` phrases average within-task score 0.992 — only 8 of 156 tasks have any phrase beating the training wording by >0.15, and most of those by cosmetics. Within-task, extra length correlates −0.20 with score even among sane phrasings, added articles ("has the") −0.26, and colour words not in the task's identity −0.34. The old book's biggest plain-input losses were pure meddling: lift→take on a fine base (−2.29), lift→pick-up-from (−2.93), articles added to telegraphic originals (−0.2 to −0.4 each). Rule 2 is the gate; it deliberately demands character-for-character copying because pure case/period edits measured as noise (mean ≈ +0.01 over ~15 pairs) while article/word edits measured negative. The gate knowingly forfeits four measured wins on gate-passing inputs (upper→top +2.07, colour-dropping +1.26 and +0.71, "from top down"→"from top to bottom" +0.42); I judged the many small consistent losses heavier than these few confounded wins — see probes below.

3. **Rule 3 is kept verbatim from the best book** — it is the only rule with a book-level measurement behind it (+0.102 at iter_00), and the regime split shows where it earns it: real rewrites of adversarial bases average **+1.65** (median +1.25, 24 pos / 5 neg). Rules 2 and 4–7 exist to confine it to that regime and to the defective fraction of natural inputs (politeness, passives like "The pot should be placed in the sink." → +0.23/+0.37, fronted goals, state-phrasings — "make sure the microwave is closed" rewrites +0.06/+0.07, and the bank's worst natural for its task is the periphrastic "Move the bowl so it is no longer sitting on the plate.", −1.26 below short forms).

4. **Minimal surgery beats restructuring** (rule 4): dropping only "Please" scored +0.00 and +0.54 on two bases whose full restructurings scored −0.28 and (in one lucky draw) +3.05; the "end effector" task is the cautionary tale — its odd original scores 8.72 and even jargon-preserving variants score 7.48, while every fluent rendering collapses below 0.6.

5. **Naming beats describing, and everyday nouns beat rare ones** (rule 5): de-riddled rewrites won big (strawberry +1.14 vs +0.37 for the riddle-preserving version; spatula +5.6/+5.4 vs +1.25; faucet +2.79; "carton"→"box" +3.05). The washbasin case resolved a mystery: the old book kept "washbasin" and lost −2.06/−1.27, yet "Take the pot from the sink" scores 6.28 on that task — the noun, not the verb, was the poison. One counterexample (the French-fries riddle kept, +0.62 vs +0.16 named) is confounded: the "named" version also invented colours and dropped the disjunction.

6. **Keep choices and landmarks; add nothing** (rule 6): across 15 "or"-tasks, phrases keeping "or" average score 0.775 vs 0.566 without (n≈155 each); every disjunction-dropping rewrite lost (sushi −1.86/−1.63, eggplant −0.51/−0.44, broccoli −0.38, pepper −1.03). Dropping landmarks lost (−0.88 "in the cardboard fence", −0.82 "out of the pot"). Invented attributes lost or tied in every minimal pair (pink plate −0.28 vs plain, toy knife −0.25, grey-for-frontmost −0.64, invented cloth colours −0.66/−0.79, invented locations −1.87/−0.83). This resolves my iter_00 leaning toward "concretize with attributes": concretize the *noun* (rule 5), never the *attributes*.

7. **Verb list** (rule 7): non-corpus verbs lost hard ("Grip …" −2.57 where "Pick up the clear glass." won +1.67; "zip" −0.10), rare→plain swaps won small (transfer→put +0.17, pop→put +0.10), and plain→plain swaps were the disaster class (−2.06, −2.93) — hence "never replace a verb that is already plain."

Caveat: the bank has **zero gt_success rows** — everything above is the proxy channels. Where rollouts can be spent, the adversarial-regime rewrites and the leak fix are the claims worth grounding first.

Measurements to land next (probes for the bank): (a) "Lift the pot up out of the sink." and "Pick up the pot from the sink." on *pick up pot from sink* — isolates washbasin→sink vs verb choice; (b) upper↔top minimal pairs on two or three cloth/edge tasks to decide whether spatial-word canonicalisation earns a rule (currently one +2.07 pair); (c) colour-stripped variants on tasks whose task name lacks colour ("Put the cup on the plate.", "Put the beet in the pot.", "Put the lid on the pot.") to decide whether rule 6 should protect base colours or drop non-identity ones; (d) disjunction-preserving minimal rewrites "Move the sushi over to the pot or the pan." and "Put the eggplant into the pan or the pot." — my rules' predicted outputs for two measured losers; (e) "Flatten the blue cloth from top to bottom." style from-A-to-B completions on other fold/unfold tasks (+0.42 once) to see if the pattern generalises before it becomes a rule.
