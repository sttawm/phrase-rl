===RULES===
1. Rewrite the instruction as a short, plain imperative that keeps the same objects and goal.
2. Exception: if the instruction is already a plain imperative of about ten words or fewer that names each object by its specific everyday noun, output it unchanged apart from any color addition rule 5 allows — do not swap its verb, synonyms, word order, or punctuation.
3. Keep the instruction's spatial relation exactly as given (on, in, into, onto, across); never swap one for another, and never add position detail the instruction does not state, such as "the center of" or "the edge of".
4. Replace technical, rare, or roundabout object references with the plain everyday word: a ramekin or small ceramic dish becomes "the white bowl"; any cola, soda, or fizzy-drink container becomes "the coke can"; eating silverware becomes "the spoon"; a textile, fabric, or woven material becomes "the towel"; a vague reference like "the orange vegetable" or "the yellow one" becomes the specific noun it points to.
5. You may add a color word only when it is the object's own well-known color: "the orange carrot", "the red coke can", "the black keyboard", "the black wheel", "the white bowl". Never add a color the instruction does not state to anything else — do not guess the color of plates, cloths, blocks, or baskets.
6. Strip politeness, questions, second-person address, and narrative framing entirely, but keep any real constraint on the motion (such as "without moving the other objects") as a short clause.
===RATIONALE===
Rule 1 is kept word for word: it is the measured core, and both measurement rounds show its gains are real but concentrated. In this round's paired rollouts the book earned +17.8 points per rewrite on adversarial/verbose bases (n=15) while LOSING 3.1 on natural bases (n=16) and 11.1 on originals (n=3). Every new rule exists to keep the adversarial gains while shutting off the two loss channels: churn on already-clean inputs and invented scene detail.

Rule 2 (new, the no-op guard) targets the churn channel directly. The worst natural-regime deltas were all rewrites of inputs that were already fine: a synonym swap "standing up"→"upright" cost −50, "Grab that red aluminum can..."→green-plate rewrite −33.3, "Stack the green on the yellow."→"Put..." −16.7, "put the coke down on the plate" trimmed −16.7, and the fragment "carrot on keyboard" verbified −33.3 in this draw. Under the guard, all of those inputs but the fragment pass through untouched. The measurement that tests this claim is the book's next paired-outcomes file: the natural and original regimes should move from −3.1/−11.1 toward zero while adversarial stays near +17.

Rule 3 (new) encodes the single cleanest failure ever measured: the applier twice mutated the wheel relation, and the probe I asked for came back — "put the carrot in the center of the wheel" scored 0.0 (0/12) against 16.7 for the plain form. Note it deliberately also blocks "on"→"on top of", which is unproven rather than harmful; the probe to relax it would be "put the carrot on top of the wheel" measured against the dense "put the carrot on the wheel" (16.7, n=126).

Rules 4 and 5 (new) split attribute handling on a line the evidence now supports. Decoding rare nouns to everyday ones is the book's biggest single win — the ramekin→"white bowl" rewrite gained +66.7, and on dense ground truth "put the coke can in the white bowl" sits at 58.3% versus 16.7% for "put the coke can in the ramekin". Colors are licensed only when intrinsic to the object, because that is exactly where dense gt says they pay: plain→"black wheel" 16.7→38.9, plain→"black keyboard" 13.9→27.8, carrot→"orange carrot" 38.9→52.8 (all n≥60). Guessed scene colors are where the book bled: the invented "green plate" cost −33.3, and — resolving a conflict with my earlier logit-based read — the proxy logit ranked "blue towel" as the spoon task's best phrase (+0.68 logit) while real rollouts rank it BELOW plain "towel" (38.9 vs 44.4); ground truth wins, so scene-color guessing is banned even when the logit likes it. Rule 2 defers to rule 5 so that the one evidenced enrichment (intrinsic colors) still applies to clean inputs instead of being frozen out by the guard.

Rule 6 (new) makes explicit what rule 1 implied and the corpus confirms (zero politeness, zero second person, zero modality above the 5-count floor in 17k training instructions), and its keep-the-constraint clause is measured: the two stack rewrites that preserved "without disturbing / keeping in place" as a short clause each gained +16.7, and the long gripper-decode gained +50 — evidence that "short" must not mean discarding real content.

Claims I am NOT making, and the probes that would earn them: "set/lay" verbs look strong on real rollouts ("set the spoon down on the towel" 72.2 vs "put..." 44.4; "set the coke on the plate" 75.0) but verb flips netted negative in paired rewrites, so verbs stay untouched — measuring "set the carrot down on the plate" and "put the silver spoon on the towel" (silver is the corpus's dominant material word, 2,107 occurrences, never yet tried on the spoon) would tell the next iteration whether a verb rule or a material rule deserves a slot. Likewise "cube" vs "block" on the stack task disagrees between gt (fragment "green cube on yellow cube" 58.3 vs "green block on yellow block" 25.0) and this round's probes (block forms at 50.0); that stays unruled until one more paired measurement settles it.
