===RULES===
1. Rewrite the instruction as a short, plain imperative that keeps the same objects and goal.
2. Output only the rewritten instruction itself, as one line of plain text — no labels or headers (never "Thinking Process:"), no quotes around it, no reasoning, no commentary before or after.
3. First decide whether the instruction needs rewriting at all. If it is twelve words or fewer and contains no politeness, no addressee talk, and no roundabout description, copy it out unchanged — same capitalisation, punctuation, articles, verbs ("set" stays "set", "grab" stays "grab"), colours, and small words ("up", "over", "down"), even if it is telegraphic or ungrammatical. Copying is the normal case and rewriting the exception. Exactly two edits are permitted on a copied instruction, and nothing else: (a) completing a truncated direction into the full "from A to B" form ("from top down" → "from top to bottom"); (b) the colour insertion licensed in rule 6.
4. Start the rewrite with the action verb. Delete politeness, requests, addressee talk, and framing clauses entirely: "please", "kindly", "could you", "I would appreciate", "you must", "assuming/provided that …", "in order to …", "proceed to …", "notwithstanding …".
5. Keep the instruction's own verb-and-preposition frame exactly ("pick up X", "take X off Y", "put X in/on Y", "fold X from A to B"); never restate the action as its outcome ("take the bowl off the plate", not "move the bowl so it is no longer on the plate"). Only when the action itself is described in a roundabout way ("increase its altitude", "hoist it clear of the surface", "ensure it comes to rest upon") translate it into one single clause built on a plain verb — prefer "put/place X on/in Y" (or fold, unfold, open, close where that is the action). Do not expand a one-goal instruction into "pick up X and put it on Y", and do not choose "move", "take", or "transfer" where "put" or "place" says the same thing.
6. Never invent detail: no sizes, no materials, no "toy", no objects or places the instruction does not mention, and never guess a colour from the object's type. Never replace a stated word with a different one: "sweet potato" stays "sweet potato", "aluminum" stays "aluminum", "coke" stays "coke" (never "soda" or "cola"), "wheel" stays "wheel" (never "tire"); vague nouns are real nouns to keep — "the red object" stays "the red object", and object, thing, item, figure, one are words to preserve, not descriptions to resolve. One addition is licensed: when the task context you are given states or shows an object's true colour, you may insert that colour directly before that object's noun ("the keyboard" → "the black keyboard", especially the goal object) — insert only, change no other word, and when unsure add nothing.
7. When an object is named only by a description, or by an unusual word few people would use for it, replace it with the single everyday kitchen word it means: "the implement used for lifting food" → "the spatula", "the transparent vessel used for drinking" → "the glass cup", "the ramekin" → "the bowl", "the bin" → "the basket". Prefer ordinary nouns (pot, pan, lid, cup, bowl, spatula, fork, cloth, towel, basket, drawer, fridge, sink, burner, cutting board); never "vessel", "receptacle", "container", "implement", "fabric". An ordinary stated noun is not unusual — leave it alone.
8. Keep every stated spatial detail in plain words: left/right, top/bottom, corner, edge, "from A to B", in/on/under/off, where the object starts ("out of the pot", "from the basket"), and both halves of an "or" choice ("the pot or the pan"). Keep "on top of" whole — never shorten "on top of the plate" to "on the plate" — and turn fancy forms plain ("atop" → "on top of"). Aim for six to nine words; shorten by cutting filler words only, never the location, the source, or the goal.
===RATIONALE===
This iteration is the first with same-draw rollout pairs and a rollout-heavy bank (347 of 524 rows carry gt_success), so several proxy-era judgments got their real test. The book's paired outcomes: **whole +15.9 rollout points per rewrite (n=23), adversarial +25.0 (n=14), natural +1.9 (n=9)**. The gains are where rules 4/5/7 fire; the natural regime is a wash with huge variance — its single worst row (−66.7: "Grab that red aluminum can and set it on the plate." → "Grab the red soda can and put it on the plate.", n=18) is a needless edit of a copy-eligible base that also traded "aluminum" for "soda". Every change below aims at that variance without touching what produces the +25.

**Kept word for word: rules 1, 2, 4.** Rule 1's shape is confirmed by the length curve in ground truth (task-demeaned gt: 6–7 words +2.3, 8–9 words +5.5, 10–12 words −3.7, 13+ words −15.8). Rule 2 has perfect adherence and zero cost. Rule 4's politeness strips keep winning in pairs ("Please shift the carrot onto the plate." → +16.7) and drive the adversarial +25.

**Rule 6 is the substantive change: the flat colour ban is inverted into a narrow insertion licence, while the anti-trade core is strengthened.** The rollout minimal pairs are now numerous and mostly n≥60: adding a scene-true colour to the goal noun helps — "black keyboard" pairs average about +10 (nine pairs, e.g. set +16.7, put +13.9, place +25.0), "black wheel" +22.2, "yellow basket" +16.6, "green plate" +11 to +14 on three of five pairs — and phrases with two colour words sit +4.7 above their task mean versus −2.3 for colour-free ones. The same data shows the licence's boundary: a scene-FALSE colour is poison ("yellow plate" −16.6 and −19.5), and word TRADES lose — coke→cola −13.9, coke→soda −11.1, wheel→tire −8 to −19, keyboard→keys −11.1, plus the −66.7 aluminum→soda pair above. Hence: insert a known-true colour, never guess, never replace anything. The old ban's other half (preserve vague nouns) stays: the resolve-vs-keep pairs are thin and split ("green one"→"green block" −16.7 at n=6; "orange vegetable"→"carrot" +16.7 at n=12), so stability wins. Test for the licence: the next rewrite_outcomes should show insertion-only edits on clean bases with positive mean, and zero trade edits; the controlled pair to land is "set the carrot on the keyboard" vs "set the carrot on the black keyboard" rewritten under this book.

**Rule 5 reworded in its second half only: translation now targets one clause, not "pick up X and put it".** Two-clause phrases sit −9.8 below their task mean (56 rows, weight 2850) against +1.7 for single-clause; opener families move −10.5, take −14.7, transfer −17.5, pick −3.5 versus put +1.4, place +6.2, set +10.5, lay +4.6; and the paired outcome "Grab … and put …" −66.7 sits beside single-clause repairs that gained (+33 to +66.7). The frame-keeping first half is unchanged in meaning — stated frames still stay — only the applier's free choice during translation is constrained. Test: ornate one-goal bases should now come back as "Put/Place the X on/in the Y"; the pair "pick up the carrot and put it on the plate" (gt 16.7, thin) vs "put the carrot on the plate" (38.9, n=78) deserves a higher-n re-measure.

**Rule 3 simplified for adherence, with its two licences enumerated.** The audit found the copy guard executed on roughly half of eligible inputs, and this iteration's natural-regime pairs price the disobedience: edits of copy-eligible bases average ~0 but range −66.7 to +50 — pure variance the guard converts to zero, minus the colour upside the rule 6 licence now captures legitimately. The rule is shorter, states copying as the normal case, and closes with an exhaustive licence list so "the only edit permitted" can't silently grow. Test: verbatim-copy rows (modulo colour insertion) should appear at roughly the eligible rate in the next outcomes file.

**Rule 7 extended from descriptions to odd nouns.** Six same-draw noun swaps off "ramekin" all gained: →bowl +19.5, →pot +27.8, →dish +30.6, →cup +41.7, →container +50.0, →basin +55.6 (all n=64), and →bowl is the licensed everyday word; "bin"→"basket" +38.9. The closing sentence ("an ordinary stated noun is not unusual") is the fence against rule 6: it keeps this licence off "wheel", "dish", "coke", where swaps measured negative or noisy. The never-list keeps "container" despite its +50 here — one scene's quirk doesn't overturn the register argument, but it goes on the probe list: "put the coke can in the bowl" vs "…in the container" vs "…in the ramekin" (the ramekin probe already sits at 16.7).

**Rule 8 reworded in two details.** "On top of" is now explicitly protected because the applier compressed it twice: gt says "put the carrot on top of the plate" 52.8 (n=96) vs "put the carrot on the plate" 38.9 (n=78), with the spoon task showing +8 to +14 across four pairs (one coke-task pair runs −19.4 the other way — the rule protects stated detail, it does not add any). "Atop" → "on top of" is the measured example (25.0 vs 52.8). The length target tightens from "about ten" to "six to nine" per the length curve above. The source-clause and or-choice protections carry over unchanged in substance: the new source probe measured neutral ("move the carrot from the table to the plate" 41.7 vs 38.9 baseline) and the or-probes split (+13.9 spoon, −13.9 carrot), so there is no case for change in either direction.

**Conflicts resolved explicitly.** (1) Old rule 6 vs this bank's colour evidence: the ban loses; rollout minimal pairs at n≥60 outweigh the proxy-era pairs behind the ban, and the licence is written insertion-only so the ban's real content — no hallucination, no trades — survives strengthened. (2) Rule 6 vs rule 7 on the noun boundary: vague superordinates (object/thing/item/figure/one) are rule 6's to keep; rare specific nouns (ramekin, bin) are rule 7's to translate; ordinary nouns belong to neither and stay. (3) The copy guard vs the colour licence: resolved by making the licence one of exactly two named exceptions inside rule 3.

**Noise discipline.** Single pairs at n=64 swing ±15 in this bank (put↔place↔set swaps go both directions across tasks), so every change above rests on aggregates or repeated pairs, and none of the single-pair curiosities (basin +55.6, "towel or the cloth" +13.9, "Convey" at 50) earned rule text.
