===RULES===
1. Rewrite the instruction as a short, plain imperative that keeps the same objects and goal.
2. Output only the rewritten instruction itself, as one line of plain text — no labels or headers (never "Thinking Process:"), no quotes around it, no reasoning, no commentary before or after.
3. First decide whether the instruction needs rewriting at all. If it is twelve words or fewer and contains no politeness, no addressee talk, and no roundabout description, copy it out unchanged — same capitalisation, punctuation, articles, nouns, colours, and small words ("up", "over", "down"), even if it is telegraphic or ungrammatical. Copying is the normal case and rewriting the exception. Exactly three edits are permitted on a copied instruction, and nothing else: (a) completing a truncated direction into the full "from A to B" form ("from top down" → "from top to bottom"); (b) the insertions licensed in rule 6; (c) if the instruction has no verb at all ("green block on yellow block"), prefix "put" or "place" and change nothing else.
4. Start the rewrite with the action verb. Delete politeness, requests, addressee talk, and framing clauses entirely: "please", "kindly", "could you", "I would appreciate", "you must", "assuming/provided that …", "in order to …", "proceed to …", "notwithstanding …".
5. Keep the instruction's own verb-and-preposition frame ("pick up X", "take X off Y", "put X in/on Y", "fold X from A to B"); never restate the action as its outcome ("take the bowl off the plate", not "move the bowl so it is no longer on the plate"). When the action is described in a roundabout way ("increase its altitude", "hoist it clear of the surface", "ensure it comes to rest upon"), rewrite it as ONE clause with the shape verb + the object + preposition + the goal, containing no "and" — "Put the carrot on the plate.", never "Pick up the carrot and put it on the plate.", even when the original narrates grasping first: the grasp is implied. Choose that verb from put, place, stack, fold, unfold, open, close; never open a rewrite with "move", "take", "transfer", "grasp", or "convey" when one of those fits.
6. Every noun and adjective of the instruction must reappear unchanged in the rewrite, unless rule 4 deleted its whole clause or rule 7 translated it: "sweet potato" stays "sweet potato", "aluminum" stays "aluminum", "coke" stays "coke" (never "soda"), "cube" stays "cube" (never "block"), "wheel" stays "wheel" (never "tire"). Vague nouns are real nouns to keep — "the red object" stays "the red object", and object, thing, item, figure, one are words to preserve, not descriptions to resolve ("the green one" never becomes "the green block"). Never invent detail: no sizes, no materials, no "toy", no objects or places the instruction does not mention. Two insertions are licensed, both only when the task context you are given states or shows the fact: you may insert an object's colour as one word from black, white, red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple, directly before that object's noun ("the keyboard" → "the black keyboard"); and when a reference has no noun at all ("the green"), you may add the object's category noun from context ("the green cube"). Insert only; change no other word. If the context does not state or show it, add nothing — a plausible colour is not a known colour, and a wrong one costs more than none.
7. Ordinary stated nouns stay as they are — pot, pan, lid, cup, bowl, plate, spatula, fork, cloth, towel, basket, drawer, sink, burner, wheel, keyboard are never swapped for near-synonyms. But when an object is named only by a description, or by a word people rarely say in a kitchen (ramekin, vessel, receptacle), replace it with the single everyday word it means: "the implement used for lifting food" → "the spatula", "the transparent vessel used for drinking" → "the glass cup", "the ramekin" → "the bowl", "the bin" → "the basket". Never write "vessel", "receptacle", "container", "implement", or "fabric" in a rewrite.
8. Keep every stated spatial detail in plain words: left/right, top/bottom, corner, edge, "from A to B", in/on/under/off, where the object starts ("out of the pot", "from the basket"), and both halves of an "or" choice ("the pot or the pan"). Keep "on top of" whole — never shorten "on top of the plate" to "on the plate" — and turn fancy forms plain ("atop" → "on top of"). Aim for six to nine words; shorten by cutting filler words only, never the location, the source, or the goal.
