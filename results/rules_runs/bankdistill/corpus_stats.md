## Shape of the corpus

Instructions are short and formulaic. Summing the published counts, the n≥50 band alone holds ≈169,600 tokens, and an estimated 6–9k more sit in the sub-50 tail, giving ≈177,000 running tokens in total and a mean length of about 10 words per instruction (approximate, derived from those sums). The sample bears this out: lines run from two or three words ("opens the drawer.", "flip salt upright", "doing nothing") up to around twenty ("Take the blue object out of the silver pot and put it on the upper right side of the stove"), with most in the 6–14 range. The vocabulary is tiny for the corpus size — 2,192 types over ≈177k tokens, a type–token ratio near 1.2% (approximate) — and the ten most frequent types (the, of, to, move, put, on, left, right, table, cloth) account for roughly half of all running text.

The canonical sentence is a single subjectless imperative: verb, definite object noun phrase, prepositional goal — "move the red spoon to the top right of the burner". Goals are compound spatial nominals glued together with *of* (10,330 occurrences, ≈0.6 per instruction, mostly in chains like "the left side of the table") and introduced by *to* (7,926), *on/onto* (4,692 + 133), or *in/into/inside* (1,996 + 388 + 413); *from* (2,318) marks sources, both in take-from/put-on pairs and in the fold family's "from A to B" pattern ("unfold the cloth from bottom left to the top right").

Openings are verb-initial in well over nine sampled lines in ten (sample-based). By lemma family across the whole corpus: move/moved/moves/moving ≈8,300 occurrences, put ≈5,000, place ≈2,500, take ≈1,300, pick ≈1,000, fold+unfold ≈700, remove ≈400 — upper bounds as opener counts, since coordinated second verbs ("take … and put …") are included. Three inflectional modes coexist: the imperative dominates; past-tense narration ("moved the silver pot to the left side of the table") covers very roughly one instruction in ten (moved 1,269, placed 207, picked 88, took 83, removed 63, plus sub-50 forms — approximate, since one line can carry two past verbs); and a small third-person or gerund mode ("opens the drawer.", "folding cloth from bottom to top left."). A handful open with the theme instead ("the handkerchief is let the robot arm drop into the table").

Determiners are definite or absent. *The* occurs 39,715 times — about 2.3 per instruction — while *a* occurs 432 times and *an* fewer than 50, so under 3% of instructions contain an indefinite article, and some of those "an"s are typos for *and* ("put corncob in pot or pan an put pot or pan on stove"). That same line shows the telegraphic article-less register that runs through the corpus ("move green block to the right side of table").

Multi-step instructions are the minority. *And* occurs 3,560 times, so at most ~21% of lines contain it, and most of those are two clauses about a single object ("Take the pepper out of the bowl and put it on the towel to dry."). Genuinely two-goal instructions form a visible but small template family ("put strawberry on plate and stuffedduck in pot or pan in sink") — a few percent at most (approximate). *Then* sits in the 5–49 band, so explicit sequencing words are rare; order is carried by clause order alone.

The text is noisy crowd-written English with a non-English fringe. Misspellings clear the 5-occurrence floor: *clothe* (50) for cloth, *banner/bunner/buner* for burner ("moved the silver pot to the top right banner"), *bowel* for bowl, *folk* for fork, *sliver* for silver, *yelow/yellon*, *righ/rigth*, *moove*, *upthe*. Capitalisation and terminal punctuation are inconsistent in the sample (including all-caps lines like "PICK UP THE BANANA AND PUT ON THE ORANGE TOWEL"), though the lowercasing tokeniser makes this unmeasurable from the table. Roughly 1–2% of instructions are French, Spanish, or Portuguese (a rough estimate from la 124, le 76, de 72, plus a 5–49 band thick with *mueve, coloca, estufa, quemador, gaveta, moveu, gauche, tiroir*) — "Le robot déplacer le tissu à gauche de la table" — and at least a handful are Malagasy ("mamelatra ilay lamba"; *lamba* clears the floor). Stray single letters (*l*, *d*, *e*) and fragments (*rieur*, *cot* + *e* from "côté") are what accented words become under the ASCII-letters tokeniser.

## Register

Objects are named by category noun and disambiguated first by **colour**. The colour field totals ≈11,100 tokens excluding *silver* (blue 2,742, yellow 2,153, red 1,758, green 1,696, orange 1,171, white 666, purple 470, down through gray 57) — about 0.64 per instruction, approximate because *orange* and *green* double as foods. Typical lines: "put the blue brush on top of the blue cloth", "Take the red spoon and put it on the yellow cloth." Occasionally colour stands alone as the whole reference: "move yellow to the bottom corner of the table on the left".

**Material** is essentially one word: *silver* (2,107), overwhelmingly attached to the pot and its kin (lid, bowl, cup) in the sample. Beyond it the field is thin — metal 213, wooden 76, plastic 58, steel 54, *stainless* under 50 ("Take the stainless steel pot and put it on the blue towel").

**Position** is the largest descriptive system of all: the fourteen core spatial words (left 4,637, right 4,428, top 3,403, side 3,032, bottom 2,058, corner 1,190, upper 958, middle 827, front 783, edge 714, lower 713, center 433 + centre 78, back 254) sum to ≈23,500 tokens, about 1.4 per instruction (approximate; *back* includes "put back"). Position both identifies objects — "put the blue knife in the orange pot on the lower right burner" — and defines goals relative to landmark objects via between 463, above 329, behind 303, near 296, next(-to) 245, below 178, beside 123: "Put the pot on the left of the avocado." Possessive location ("Put the strawberry in the table's upper right corner.") exists but is rare (*table's*, 5–49).

**Vague superordinates** are routine: object 1,553, thing 320, figure 180, vessel 163, item 91, plus sub-50 *stuff/something* — ≈2,300 tokens, roughly one instruction in seven or eight, usually colour-plus-vague: "puts the green thing in the drawer", "pick up the white object and place it on the top right of the napkin".

**Shape** terms carry the block-play subdomain: block 868, cube 713, arch 394, cylinder 261, rectangular 252, rectangle 215, triangle 78, square 54, with a rare tail of cone, sphere, hexagon, pyramid, prism, cuboid, and even *parallelepiped* — "put the square with hole in the middle on top of the blue arch".

**Function and parts** are minor. Function survives mainly in compounds whose modifiers sit under 50 — washing machine (washing 100, machine 127), frying pan, cutting board, measuring cup, drying rack. Part-based metonymy appears at the 5–49 level through *handle/handled*: "moved the yellow handled knife to the edge of the right burner", "Move the blue-handled scoop to the upper-mid part of the stove."

**Size and brand** are rare to absent. *Small, little, big, long, tall, thin* all sit in the 5–49 band, and *large*, *bigger*, *smaller* fall below the floor even though the sample contains "move the bigger can to the right of the smaller can". No brand or product name clears the floor; cans are "the red and yellow cans" or "the salt can", never a label.

The same referent goes by many names with a strong head term: cloth 4,102 / towel 1,218 / napkin 199 / rag 62 (handkerchief, fabric, *tissu* under 50; the sample even has sub-floor "sufuria" for pot); burner 1,941 / stove 994 / cooker 56 (hob, cooktop, stovetop, induction, heater at 5–49). Finally, some lines betray a video-caption origin rather than an addressed command: *robot* (57) appears only in third person, and *video, image(s), sequence, progresses* all clear the floor.

## Notable absences

One calibration first: "absent" below means not in the table — fewer than 5 occurrences among 17,297, not necessarily zero. The printed sample itself contains below-floor words (*large, bigger, smaller, both, whilst, product, sufuria*), so the floor hides real stragglers; no claim here is of a literal zero count.

- **Politeness, addressee, and modality.** I looked for *please, kindly, thanks, you, your, could, would, should, may, must*: none are in the table. *Can* (694) is the tin — every sample use is the container. There are no questions and no second person anywhere; the corpus describes episodes rather than addressing an agent.
- **Manner, care, and speed.** *Carefully, gently, slowly, quickly, fast, softly*: none. The only manner language is geometric or degree-based — *slightly, diagonally, straight, directly* (all 5–49), "little bit", *just* (87). Nothing in the corpus ever says how forcefully or cautiously to move something.
- **Negation and constraints.** *Don't, avoid, without, never*: none. *No, not, nothing* exist only at 5–49. Prohibitions and side-constraints ("without touching…", "don't knock over…") do not occur above the floor.
- **Quantifiers and measurement.** *All, every, each, some, several, many, few*: none (*both* is below floor). No unit words — *cm, inch, inches, centimeters, degrees* are all absent — and number words are nearly so (two 126; one, four at 5–49; *three* below floor). Digits are not tokenised so cannot be checked, but the spelled-out record shows displacement is never metric; magnitude is "slightly" or "little bit".
- **Sequencing and logic.** *After, while, until, when, if, unless, first, second, finally*: none (*then* and *before* only 5–49). Even two-clause instructions rely on bare *and* and clause order; conditional and temporal subordination do not appear.
- **Deixis.** *Here, there*: none; *this/that* only 5–49. Every location is spelled out absolutely or relative to a named landmark, consistent with captions written about a recorded scene rather than utterances in a shared physical space.
- **Verbs beyond relocation.** *Pour, stir, cut, chop, mix, press, squeeze, twist, screw, sprinkle, spread, shake, throw*: none. Cooking itself is absent — *cook, boil, heat* are missing (*cooking, cooker, frying, heater* occur only as modifiers or nouns), and *clean* and *dry* fall below the floor even though towels, sponges, soap, and a scrubber are all present as objects. The kitchen is scenery: things are moved, folded, and opened or closed (open 59, close 69, lid 451, cover 106), never operated, cooked with, or cleaned with.
- **Appliance operation and inventory.** *On* (4,692) is spatial, and no turn-on/turn-off language exists (*turn* and *switch* sit at 5–49, the latter as the noun "burner switch" in the sample). *Fridge, refrigerator, kettle, toaster, dishwasher* never clear the floor, while stove 994, sink 220, microwave 161, and the washing machine do.
- **Comparatives, superlatives, and embodiment.** No -est form clears the floor — probes for *closest, nearest, biggest, largest* found nothing; *closer* and *further* sit at 5–49 and *bigger/smaller* below the floor, so objects are almost never selected by relative size or proximity ranking. *Gripper, claw, effector*: none; *arm* and *hand* only at 5–49.

Taken together, the absences outline the corpus's conventions: terse, self-contained captions of single short manipulations, with objects fixed by colour, category, and place — no dialogue framing, no manner or safety qualifiers, no quantification or measurement, and no logic beyond a single "and".
