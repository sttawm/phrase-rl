===RULES===

1. OUTPUT FORMAT (always). Reply with exactly one line containing only the final instruction text — no reasoning, no labels, no quotation marks, no explanation, no second line. Never describe what you did or why.

2. NORMALIZE (always, on every output, including untouched copies). Lowercase the entire line and delete any final punctuation mark (period, exclamation point, question mark). This is the ONLY edit permitted when no Rule 4 trigger fires. Never re-add capitals or end punctuation.

3. DEFAULT IS COPY. Test the input against the trigger list in Rule 4. If NO trigger fires, output the input with only Rule 2 normalization — do not swap, add, or delete any word; do not change the verb, prepositions, articles, colors, or word order. Fluent single-goal sentences are protected regardless of their opening verb: sentences starting with grab, slide, drop, lay, stick, rest, take, or a two-verb chain of the form "pick up / grab / take the X and put/place/set it ..." are COPY cases, never rewrite cases.

4. TRIGGERS. Rewrite only if at least one of these is true. The words "please" and "kindly" alone never trigger a rewrite — they are just deleted by Rule 5.
   a. WRAPPER: the input is a question, or contains "can you", "could you", "would you", "for me", "please", "kindly", "it is requested", "it would be appreciated". Action: Rule 5 strip only.
   b. CAVEAT/PADDING: contains a "without ..." or "avoiding ..." constraint clause, or the padding "the surface of", "the center of", "the confines of". Action: Rule 5 strip only.
   c. BANNED VERB: the main placement verb is in the banned family of Rule 7. Action: in-place verb and preposition repair per Rules 7 and 11.
   d. RARE NOUN: an object or target is named by a specialized jargon word per Rule 9. Action: in-place noun swap per Rule 9.
   e. DESCRIPTOR: an object or target is named by a multi-word description or riddle instead of its everyday name. Action: Rule 6 rebuild.
   f. LONG: 13 or more words, counted after any Rule 5 stripping. Action: Rule 6 rebuild.
   g. MULTI-CLAUSE: contains a comma or semicolon, more than one sentence, or three or more verbs. Action: Rule 6 rebuild.
   h. BROKEN OPENER: the first word is not a verb (starts with e.g. "assuming", "given", "should", "it", "the", "rather", "unto", "while", "you"), or the sentence is passive ("must be", "is to be", "be made to", "be taken"). Action: Rule 6 rebuild.
   i. MISSING DISCRIMINATOR: two same-type objects are involved (e.g. stacking) and the line does not tell them apart by color or full noun ("stack the cubes", "cube on cube", "the yellow one"), or an object is only a pronoun ("it", "there"). Action: Rule 6 rebuild.
   A telegraphic line without articles that still names both objects and any needed discriminators (e.g. "spoon on towel", "green cube on yellow cube") fires NO trigger — copy it unchanged.

5. STRIP, DON'T RE-TEMPLATE. For triggers a and b: delete only the wrapper, courtesy, caveat, and padding words, recast as a direct imperative, and keep every remaining word, the verb choice, the nouns, and the word order exactly as given (example shape: "could you grab that spoon and set it on the cloth for me?" becomes "grab that spoon and set it on the cloth"). Delete: question openers, "please", "kindly", "for me", any "without ..."/"avoiding ..." clause to its end, and locative padding so the preposition attaches directly to the target noun ("on the surface of the plate" becomes "on the plate"). After stripping, re-test triggers c–i; if none fire, this stripped line is the output.

6. REBUILD TEMPLATE (triggers e, f, g, h, i). Identify from the instruction plus the scene trace the OBJECT (thing to move) and the TARGET (where it goes), then emit exactly:
      <verb> the [color] <object> <preposition> the [color] <target>
   - verb: the input's main placement verb if it is on the safe list of Rule 7, otherwise "put". For one block/cube going onto another, keep "stack" if the input used it, and use "on top of" as the preposition.
   - nouns per Rules 8 and 9, colors per Rule 10, preposition per Rule 11, no added content per Rule 12.
   - 4 to 10 words, exactly one clause, one placement verb, no comma.

7. VERBS. Banned family — always replace with "put": move, transfer, shift, relocate, convey, transport, bring, carry, leave, deposit, position, situate, elevate, hoist, superimpose, arrange. When trigger c fires on an otherwise clean sentence, replace the verb in place, keep everything else, and fix its preposition per Rule 11 ("transfer the carrot over to the wheel" becomes "put the carrot on the wheel"). Safe verbs — keep exactly whichever the input used: put, place, set, stack, slide, lay, drop, stick, rest, grab. put, place, and set are measured equals: never swap one for another. Keep "stack" for block-on-block inputs. In a two-verb chain, the second (placement) verb is the main verb.

8. PLAIN NAMES, TRACE-RESOLVED. Name every object by the shortest common word an ordinary person would use for what the scene trace shows — the everyday name, never a description, riddle, or category phrase (a description like "the orange root vegetable" or "the device used for typing" becomes the trace's everyday noun, e.g. "carrot", "keyboard"). Keep a brand or distinctive word the input already uses ("coke" stays "coke", never "cola", "soda", or bare "can"). Never apply this in reverse, and never invent a description for a plainly named object.

9. RARE NOUN SWAP (surgical, trigger d). If a noun is a specialized kitchenware or technical term that ordinary speech would not use for the object the trace shows (example of the principle: "ramekin" for what the trace shows as a small white bowl), replace just that noun with the trace's everyday noun plus the trace's color word for it, resolved at apply time ("the ramekin" becomes "the white bowl" only because the trace says the bowl is white). Apply ONLY to genuinely specialized words. Do NOT replace ordinary household words that merely differ from the trace's word (basin, dish rack, tub, cloth all measured as well as or better than their common siblings). Never swap one common noun for a common sibling: keep the input's plate (not dish), wheel (not tire or rim), towel (not cloth), basket (not bin), keyboard (not keys), and cube/block as given. If unsure whether a word is specialized, keep it.

10. COLORS — conditional, trace-only. Apply only when some trigger has fired; never on a pure copy. Never delete a color word the input wrote; never add a color the trace does not state; at most one plain single-word color per noun; never compound colors ("light green", "teal-green") or material words ("aluminum", "ceramic", "woven").
    - TARGET color: if the trace states the target's color and the target noun has no color word, ADD it when the target is a container the object goes inside (e.g. basket, bowl, bin, pot), or an object not made for holding things (e.g. wheel, keyboard, another cube), or has a same-shape look-alike of a different color in the scene. Do NOT add a color to a plain flat surface target (plate, towel, cloth, tray, table) that has no look-alike.
    - OBJECT color: during a Rule 6 rebuild, keep the input's color; add the trace's color only if the object noun alone is generic (can, cube, block, vegetable, one) or a same-type look-alike exists ("the can" becomes "the red can"). Do not add a color to an already-specific object noun (carrot, spoon, eggplant).

11. PREPOSITIONS. If the input's preposition is one of on, onto, on top of, atop, in, into, inside — keep it exactly. Never simplify "atop" or "on top of" down to "on", and never delete the particle "down" ("put the coke down on the plate" and "set the spoon down on the towel" are top-measured forms; "down" is not an adverb to strip). When the input's spatial wording is anything else ("to", "over to", "toward", "across", "upon the surface of", "until it makes contact with", "in the center of") or is missing, translate by geometry: object ends up inside an open container (e.g. bowl, basket, cup, pot, mug, box, bin) → "in" or "into"; object rests on top of something (e.g. plate, towel, keyboard, wheel, table, another block) → "on", with "on top of" for block-on-block stacking.

12. NEVER ADD CONTENT. Never add: constraint clauses ("without ...", "avoiding ..."), manner adverbs (carefully, swiftly, directly, securely), pose words (upright, standing, flat), size, shape, or material adjectives (small, elongated, aluminum), politeness words, or a "pick up ... and" decomposition. When rebuilding, drop any of these present in the input ("down" is exempt per Rule 11).

13. LENGTH CHECK on rebuilds. If a Rule 6 rebuild exceeds 10 words, delete in this order until at or under 10: non-color adjectives, then the object's color, then the target's color. Never delete the verb, either noun, "the", the preposition, or "down". Never change WHICH object moves or WHERE it goes; if the input is ambiguous about that, keep its ambiguity rather than guessing.

14. CONFIDENCE FALLBACK. If a trigger fired but you cannot name BOTH the object and the target using only the instruction and the scene trace, output the input unchanged except Rule 2 normalization. Never use a noun or color that appears in neither the instruction nor the trace.

15. FINAL CHECK. The output is one line, all lowercase, no end punctuation, at most one clause with one placement action. Output it per Rule 1 and stop.

===RATIONALE===
All numbers are gt_success (0–100, real rollouts) weighted by n_ctx, from evidence.csv.

- R2 (always normalize): high-n twins show a 14–19 pt case/period penalty — "put the coke can on the plate" 61.1 (n=304) vs same+period 47.2 (n=64); "place the carrot on the keyboard" 13.9 (n=78) vs "Place the carrot on the keyboard." 0.0 (n=96); "place the red cola can inside the white bowl" 61.1 (n=70) vs +period 41.7 (n=64).
- R3 (copy default): rewriting clean inputs backfires — "Grab that red aluminum can and set it on the plate." 66.7 (n=274) vs its rewrites' mean ~24; "Slide the eggplant into the yellow basket." 91.7 (n=286); "Could you grab that spoon and set it on the cloth for me?" 50.0 (n=220). Grab/slide/take-initial phrases and chains beat every template, so they are protected, not triggers.
- R4a/R5 (strip, don't re-template): "please put the coke can on the plate" 61.1 = its plain twin, so please is deletion-only; "Can you put the carrot on the wheel for me?" 0.0 (n=78) vs the stripped core 16.7 (n=348).
- R4b/g (kill-signals): "without/avoiding" clauses 0.5 wavg vs 37.5 (minimal pair 66.7 → 0.0); comma rows 5.0 vs 39.7; "surface" padding 4.7 vs 38.0; 13+ words score 0–21 on 6 of 8 tasks; passive/non-verb openers ~0–17; ornate adversarials 13.2 wavg vs 33.9 for their short-imperative rewrites.
- R7 (verbs): move-family first-word wavg 15.7 over 21 phrases vs put 43.4 / place 42.9 / set 43.2 (a three-way tie, so no preference order); "Transfer the carrot over to the wheel." 0.0 (n=300); "move the carrot to the keyboard" 0.0. Safe verbs measure at task-typical rates: "lay the spoon on the towel" 63.9, "Stack the green on the yellow." 50.0 (n=224), "Stack the green cube on top of the yellow cube." 66.7 — stack is kept, "on top of" is the best stacking frame.
- R8 (plain names): "Move the orange vegetable to the wheel." 0.0 (n=252); descriptor phrases 0.0 at n=188–246 on carrot, coke, and spoon tasks. Descriptor fatality is task-dependent (eggplant descriptors scored 100), but the rewrite direction is safe everywhere. "coke" kept beats cola/soda paraphrases (53.7 vs 28.9).
- R9 (rare vs sibling nouns): ramekin rows 0–22.2 vs same-task bowl rows 51.9 wavg; but "inside the white basin" 77.8 (n=64) is best-in-task and "yellow dish rack" 94.4–100 ≥ every basket row, so only true jargon is swapped, with the color resolved from the trace, never baked in. Sibling swaps are the costliest edit: dish 30.6 (n=262) vs plate 61.1 (n=304); bin 58.3 vs basket 80.6–97.2; tire 8.3 vs wheel 16.7.
- R10 (conditional color): correct target color helps hard/container targets — wheel 16.7 (n=348) → +black 38.9 (n=66); keyboard 13.9 → +black 27.8; basket 80.6 → +yellow 97.2 — and hurts plain surfaces: plate 61.1 (n=304) → +correct green 41.7 (n=24); towel 44.4 → +blue 38.9 (n=64). Compound colors hurt ("teal-green cube" 22.2 vs "green cube" 38.9).
- R11 (protected forms): "put the spoon atop the towel" 75.0 (n=72) and "on top of the towel" 58.3 vs "on the towel" 44.4 (n=106); "put the carrot on top of the plate" 52.8 (n=264) vs 38.9 (n=138); particle down: "put the coke down on the plate" 66.7 (n=274), "set the spoon down on the towel" 72.2 (n=64); "in the center of the wheel" 0.0.
- R4i (telegraphic): "stack the cubes" 0.0 and "cube on cube" 8.3 fail from missing discriminators, while "green cube on yellow cube" 58.3 (n=56) and "spoon on towel" 58.3 (n=76) BEAT their article-restored rebuilds (36.1–38.9 and 44.4) — so expansion fires only when discriminating information is missing, never for dropped articles alone.

===KEY EVIDENCE (distiller)===
- Case/period normalization is the only universal edit: three high-n twins show 14–19 pt penalties (61.1 vs 47.2 n=304/64; 13.9 vs 0.0 n=78/96; 61.1 vs 41.7).
- Copy-by-default is mandatory: "Grab that red aluminum can and set it on the plate." 66.7 (n=274), "Slide the eggplant into the yellow basket." 91.7 (n=286), "Could you grab that spoon and set it on the cloth for me?" 50.0 (n=220) all beat every candidate's template rewrite — rebuilds fire only on measured kill-signals (13+ words, comma/multi-clause, without-clause, passive/non-verb opener, descriptor, move-family verb, missing discriminator).
- Kill-signals verified: without-clauses 0.5 wavg vs 37.5, commas 5.0 vs 39.7, move-family 15.7 wavg vs put/place/set 43.4/42.9/43.2 (tie — no verb preference), 13+ words 0–21 on 6/8 tasks.
- Color is conditional, not blanket: correct trace color gains +22 (wheel), +14 (keyboard), +17 (basket) but LOSES −19 on plate (61.1→41.7, n=304 vs 24) and −6 on towel — add only to containers, atypical targets, and look-alikes.
- Rare-noun swap is a principle, not a mapping: ramekin 0–22 vs bowl 51.9 same-task, but "white basin" 77.8 and "yellow dish rack" 94.4–100 beat their common siblings, and sibling swaps cost 20–30 pts (dish 30.6 vs plate 61.1) — swap only true jargon, color resolved from the trace at apply time.
- Protected high-value surface forms: "atop the towel" 75.0 / "on top of" 58.3 vs "on" 44.4; particle "down" 66.7–72.2 — no strip or preposition rule may flatten them.
