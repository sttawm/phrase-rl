# INSTRUCTION REWRITE PROTOCOL
## Frozen pi0 policy — WidowX / Bridge kitchen-tabletop

**Preamble.** The executor rewrites (or passes through) natural-language instructions for a **frozen pi0 policy** operating a WidowX arm on Bridge-style kitchen-tabletop scenes. (*These deployment names — "pi0", "WidowX", "Bridge" — are externally supplied task context, not mined findings; "Bridge" in particular is a dataset name given from outside the mining evidence.*) Every rule below is derived **exclusively** from mining the policy's own training data: 213 phrase-search boards, a 49-instruction × 16-transform single-edit probe (664 deltas — not every transform was applied to every instruction, so the total is less than 49×16=784), 4 hypothesize-then-holdout freeform rounds (14 tested hypotheses), and word frequencies over the policy's 17,297 training instructions. No outside knowledge about robots or language is used or permitted. The training phrasing is empirically a **local optimum**: rules exist mainly to stop you from breaking it.

---

## Part 1 — The default is PASS-THROUGH

**Rule 1 (default = return the instruction unchanged).** Originals were unbeaten in 144/213 boards (68%, train). An always-rewrite policy that substitutes the best searched rephrase changes grip by **median −12.4%, mean −52.4%**, hurting in 68% of boards and hurting by >20% in 41% vs helping by >20% in only 4% (n=213, train). Even the oracle selective-rewrite ceiling is only **+3.6% mean** (n=213, train). Single edits average −0.0093 grip with a 6:1 large-loss-to-large-win ratio at |Δ|≥0.05 (50 losses / 8 wins, n=664, train). Freeform style-rule search refuted or nulled **14/14** holdout-tested hypotheses (max mean_delta +0.00074, n=12 each, train). Therefore: **rewrite only when Rules 5–9 affirmatively fire and no freeze in Rules 2–4 applies. When in doubt, pass through.**

**Rule 2 (freeze list — never rewrite these).**
a. **Cloth / fold / unfold / upright / directional-cloth instructions.** Unbeaten rates: unfold 100% (n=8), upright 100% (n=3), fold 85% (n=13) (train); pooled, the 24 cloth boards carrying a direction or argument kept the original in 22/24 — the only cloth beats anywhere are the two fold color-word wins (train). Cloth instructions pooled −0.0441 per edit vs −0.0059 elsewhere, a 7.5× fragility gap (n=59 vs 605, train); decomposing a fold gave the single worst delta in the probe (−0.304, train); paraphrases of unfold-cloth landed among the worst gaps in every freeform round they appeared (−0.15 to −0.26, train). The sole edit ever observed to win on fold is adding a color word (2/2 fold beats, +7.7% and +18.5%, train) — and even that is optional, never required.
b. **Originals that contain an article ("the"/"a").** 89% unbeaten (33/37) vs 63% (111/176) without, Fisher p=0.002, odds 4.8 (train).
c. **Longer originals.** Beaten originals average 4.78 tokens vs 5.77 for unbeaten (MWU p=0.0013, train). ≥6 tokens → strong pass-through prior.
d. **take / end_effector / flip families.** Unbeaten: take 81% (n=21), end_effector 75% (n=8), flip 75% (n=4) (train). For end-effector templates, keep the jargon template verbatim; the only meaningful win replaced a concatenated OOV noun inside the kept template ("stuffedrabbit"→"pink bunny", +9.8%, train).
e. **Instructions already containing spatial terms (left/right/upper/top/bottom).** On the 6 probe instructions containing these terms, added detail or reordering costs ≈6–9× the non-spatial per-edit mean (−0.0059): add_spatial −0.0342 (vs −0.0042 on non-spatial instructions), goal_state −0.0405, destination_first −0.0514 (train); compressing a spatial description is among the costliest edits (−0.1586 worst case, train); the stuck spatial-move instruction worsened under 14/16 transforms (train).

**Rule 3 (never do these edits, in any family).**
a. **Never rename an object to a visual description** outside the codename case in Rule 6: rename_visual is the worst broad transform, −0.0249 mean, 6W/23L, worst −0.169 (n=49, train).
b. **Never remove adjectives the original has**: 0 wins / 5 losses, −0.0210 mean, worst −0.0784 (n=9, train).
c. **Never drop articles the original has**: −0.0385 mean, 1W/4L, worst −0.1585 (n=6, train).
d. **Retaining the object noun is the winner norm, not a proven requirement**: 63/69 winners retained ≥1 original content noun (91%, train). The corrected data show no penalty for dropping — winners retaining zero original nouns (n=6) averaged +10.4%, and winners dropping a noun without adding one (n=11) averaged +13.7%, including the +79.8% large4fbox board (train). Default to keeping the noun (it is what 91% of winners did), but do not treat dropping as forbidden when a Rule 6–8 pattern fires.
e. **Never decompose into multi-step phrasing** (output is one line anyway): decompose −0.1135 mean on cloth, −0.0712 on spatial (train).
f. **Never use formal/rare verbs** — transfer (−0.0243), grasp (−0.0070, 0W), scrub (−0.0288), crease (−0.0267) (train); high-intent verb substitution (toss/transfer/insert/position) was refuted twice on holdout (−0.047 and −0.009, n=12 each, train).

**Rule 4 (refuted-rule blacklist — plausible-sounding edits proven useless or harmful on holdout, n=12 each, train).** Do not: specify container material ("metal pot", −0.044); strip object-state descriptions (−0.051); replace state-change verbs (crease/straighten/smooth/unroll) with direct verbs (fold/take/grab) — this was the tested edit and it scored −0.0324 (the reverse direction, direct→state-change, was never tested); add destination color/sub-region detail (−0.004 and −0.007); de-jargon "end effector" (−0.002, null); add or strip politeness for its own sake (−0.0003, null; single-edit probe −0.0005, the smallest-variance transform, n=49); add or remove color on simple objects as a standalone edit (+0.0007, null — color pays only inside a Rule-5 rewrite, see Rule 7). Do not pad: winner length +2.09 tokens is correlational only — within-pool length-grip Spearman is −0.048 (p=0.11, 137 boards, train), and elaboration's cost tracks changed content words, not added words (length-delta correlation +0.02, train).

## Part 2 — When a rewrite IS justified

**Rule 5 (eligibility gate — family first).** A rewrite may be considered only when the instruction's head verb is in the beatable set: **turn** (20% unbeaten, mean rel gain 12.5%), **close** (31%, 5.7%), **open** (43%, 10.1%); secondarily **put** (70% unbeaten), **pick** (67%), **move** (67%) when another rule (6, 8, or 9) also fires (train). Expect modest payoffs when you do rewrite: winning rewrites averaged **+11.2% mean / +7.8% median** rel grip (n=69, train), and 12/69 wins were <3%, within eval noise (screen-vs-final Spearman −0.227, train) — a rewrite that only promises a sub-3% edge is not worth the −12.4%-median downside of Rule 1. Do **not** use apparent task difficulty as a trigger: Spearman(difficulty, beatability) = −0.039, p=0.57 (n=213, train), and saturated instructions are mostly phrasing-invariant: of the 16 probe instructions with base grip ≤0.02, 13/16 never moved >0.02 under any transform (14/16 excusing a borderline 0.02001 case); the exceptions moved 0.169 (rename_visual) and 0.067 (train).

**Rule 6 (codename naturalization — the highest-yield edit).** If the instruction contains a **digit-bearing multi-flap codename** ("small4fbox"/"large4fbox") under an open/close-flap verb, replace the codename with a plain object name and keep the flap noun: all 4/4 4fbox boards were beaten ("open large4fbox flaps"→"pull the cardboard flaps open" **+79.8%**; "close large4fbox flaps"→"shut the flaps on the cardboard box" +21.2%, train); codename-replacing winners averaged **+25.3%** (n=5, train). **Scope strictly:** 1fbox-style codes mostly held (3/4 unbeaten, only beat +2.8% = noise, train), and paraphrasing "small4fbox" inside a different-family, near-solved instruction destroyed it (−0.169, train) — so naturalize codenames **only** in open/close-flap instructions; everywhere else coined tokens are load-bearing identifiers and must be copied verbatim. Concatenated OOV nouns inside kept templates (e.g. "stuffedrabbit") may be split into a natural name (+9.8%, train) — check OOV status against the Appendix.

**Rule 7 (payload of a winning rewrite = lexical specificity).** When Rules 5–6 open the door, build the rewrite from the edit types that actually won:
a. **Noun swap to a specific concrete name** — the top edit class: strict noun swaps averaged +12.9% (n=20, train); the broader any-new-noun class averaged +11.9% (n=30, train).
b. **Verb swap along the observed winner map**: open→pull, close→push/shut, put→move/place, turn→rotate, take→remove (observed winner counts 4/4+3/7+6/2/2, train); verb-changing winners gained +12.6% vs +8.3% for verb-keepers (46/69, train). Prefer concrete physical verbs, which are near-free even as lone edits (put→drop +0.0012, pick→lift +0.0025, move→slide +0.0074, train). (Any "force-direction" or "articulated-object" mechanistic reading of this map is uncited interpretation, not a mined finding.)
c. **Color/material/size modifier or named part (door, handle, lever, burner)** riding on the noun: added in roughly 32–41 of 69 winners depending on the modifier vocabulary used; modifier-adders gained more under most definitions (~+11% to +14% vs ~+7% to +11% for non-adders, train) — the direction holds, the exact split does not pin down.
d. **Articles may ride along** (49/69 winners added "the") but are not the mechanism — article-only minimal pairs were null: 549 article-difference pairs, 51% favor the article, p=0.55 (269 single-article-edit pairs: 52%, p=0.46, train).
e. **Keep prepositions plain**: in/on beat into/onto/inside in 57% of 321 single-token preposition minimal pairs (p=0.019, train); never with→using (−0.0235, train).
f. **When shortening, drop only trailing scene context, never the object**: 7/7 shorter winners kept the noun and cut qualifiers ("turn faucet front to left"→"push faucet lever left" +36.8%; "take corn out of bowl sink"→"take corn out of bowl" +6.3%, train). (Provenance note: "faucet" is absent from Appendix A, i.e. <5 occurrences in the 17,297 training instructions — consistent with a rare-train-instruction board, but this board's membership in the train phrase-search set should be verified before leaning on it.)

**Rule 8 (disjunction resolution — only with scene evidence).** If the instruction contains "or" ("pot or pan") **and the scene trace identifies which referent is present**, name that single object: 7/7 winning rewrites on 'or'-boards dropped the disjunction ("put lid on pot or pan"→named the silver pot, +19.4%, train). But disjunction boards were no more beatable than the rest (67% vs 68% unbeaten, train), so without a confident scene identification, pass through.

**Rule 9 (corpus-vocabulary check — mandatory before emitting any rewrite).** Winning rewrites moved **away from** corpus-frequent words: mean content-token corpus frequency fell 23.7→12.5, downward in 61/69 winners (Wilcoxon p<1e-4, train), and 44% (103/233) of tokens winners introduced are corpus-OOV by the appendix's literal membership rule (train) — with the caveat that 49 of the 103 are the word "the" and ~26 more are other function words the vocab file omits; content-word OOV is 23/147 (16%, train). Tokens winners dropped were high-frequency (mean 31.5, e.g. put=103-count-in-boards, train). Procedure: look up every content token of your candidate in **Appendix A**. (i) **Reject** any rewrite that replaces the original's specific words with higher-frequency Common-tier words — frequency-seeking is anti-predictive. (ii) A candidate whose introduced content tokens are lower-frequency or OOV relative to what it replaces is consistent with the winner profile. (iii) Use the appendix to flag concatenated OOV tokens for Rule 6's template-noun split. The appendix is the sole vocabulary authority.

**Rule 10 (consistency across contexts).** An instruction's rewrite decision is stable across scene contexts (club-vs-board grip Spearman 0.919, p=6e-87, n=213, train): given the same instruction again, emit the same decision. But do not trust one-shot small margins — low-fidelity screening ordered finalists at r=−0.227 with top-1 survival 13% (28/213, train); only commit to a rewrite pattern backed by the ≥10%-gain class (27/69 winners, train), i.e. the patterns in Rules 6–8.

## Part 3 — Output contract

The executor receives an **instruction** plus a **scene trace**. All reasoning is internal. The reply is **exactly one rewritten instruction** (when passing through, the original normalized to all-lowercase with no final period — the lowercase/no-period normalization takes precedence over verbatim reproduction whenever the original contains capitals or a trailing period):

- one line only
- all lowercase
- no final period
- no commentary, labels, quotes, or explanation of any kind

---

## Part 4 — Worked examples

**Example 1 — rewrite fires (Rules 5, 6, 7, 9).**
Input instruction: `open large4fbox flaps`
Scene trace: tabletop with a large brown cardboard box, four flaps closed, gripper idle above it.
Internal reasoning: head verb "open" → beatable family (43% unbeaten, +10.1% mean gain); digit-bearing 4fbox codename under a flap verb → Rule 6 naturalization (4/4 boards beaten, +25.3% class mean, this exact board +79.8%); verb map open→pull (+12.6% verb-change class); appendix check: "cardboard" and "flaps" are both corpus-OOV (neither appears in Appendix A) — consistent with the 23.7→12.5 downward-frequency winner profile; flap noun retained (the 91%-of-winners norm).
Output:
```
pull the cardboard flaps open
```

**Example 2 — pass-through (Rules 1, 2a, 2b).**
Input instruction: `fold the cloth from left to right`
Scene trace: flat blue cloth centered on the table, no other objects.
Internal reasoning: fold family is 85% unbeaten and direction/argument-bearing cloth boards kept the original in 22/24 (Rule 2a); the original contains an article (89% unbeaten class, p=0.002) and an explicit direction argument; cloth is 7.5× more edit-fragile than everything else (−0.0441 pooled); no rule in Part 2 fires (no codename, no disjunction, not open/close/turn). Default from Rule 1: emit unchanged.
Output:
```
fold the cloth from left to right
```
