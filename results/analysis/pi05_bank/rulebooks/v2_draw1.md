===RULES===
1. Default to emitting the incoming instruction unchanged. Change a word only where rule 3, rule 5 or rule 11 below explicitly licenses that change; every other rule here is a prohibition, never a licence. When two candidate outputs both satisfy the rules, emit the one closer to the original wording. Rewriting is a bet with a small upside and a large downside: word changes almost never add success, and the wrong one can remove nearly all of it.
2. Never replace the name of an object, surface or receptacle with a broader class word - a word that names a kind, container or material that several different things could answer to (for example "drink", "bottle", "carton", "can", "tin", "jar", "box", "cup", "snack", "food", "sauce", "condiment", "dish", "container", "item", "thing"). Keep the product-level or object-level name the instruction already gives ("orange juice", "ketchup", "bbq sauce", "salad dressing", "tomato sauce", "chocolate pudding", "cream cheese", "plate", "ramekin", "moka pot"). This prohibition holds however obvious the broader word looks and however few objects the scene contains.
3. Go the other way whenever the input lets you: if the instruction refers to a scene object - the thing to be moved, the destination, or a landmark inside a locating phrase - only by such a broad class word, and the scene description you are given names exactly one object of that class using a different head noun, replace the class word with the scene's name for that object and drop any colour, material, shape or size adjective the instruction had attached to the class word ("pick up the red bottle" with a scene naming ketchup becomes "pick up the ketchup"). Do not fire this rule when the scene description is absent or names no such object, when two or more scene objects answer to the class word, or when the scene's name is the instruction's own head noun carrying extra modifiers ("bowl" in the instruction, "black bowl" in the scene): in all three cases leave the wording alone rather than guessing.
4. Apart from the replacements licensed by rules 3 and 5, carry every object, surface, receptacle and fixture noun through exactly as written, even when a synonym looks obviously equivalent: plate/dish, bowl/dish, ramekin/dish, mug/cup, cabinet/cupboard, compartment/slot, moka pot/coffee pot/espresso pot, rack/stand. Rule 3 licenses the vague-to-specific direction only; swapping one specific name for another specific name, or any name for a vaguer one, is never licensed, and never rename a destination merely to make it "clearer".
5. When the instruction asks for a cooking fixture to be switched on or off and names it with one of these terms - "hot plate", "hotplate", "hob", "cooktop", "cooker", "range", "griddle", "heating element", "burner", "burners", "stove burner", "ring", "induction plate" - replace that term with the word "stove" and change nothing else in the sentence, so "switch on the electric hot plate" becomes "switch on the electric stove". Treat that list as closed: do not fire on any word outside it, never emit any word on it, never rewrite "stove" or "stovetop" into anything, and never apply this rule to a fixture that is only a destination to put something on ("put the moka pot on the hot plate" is left alone).
6. Never add a colour, material, shape or size adjective that the instruction does not already carry, and never swap one for a near-synonym ("black" to "grey" or to "dark"). No rule licenses deleting an adjective either, so leave the ones that arrive in place; rule 3's substitution is the only exception.
7. Make at most one substitution inside any one referring phrase. If rule 3 or rule 5 has replaced a word in a phrase, leave that phrase's article, remaining adjectives, selector words and governing verb exactly as they were: a second edit stacked onto a phrase you have already changed is where the largest losses occur.
8. Keep every word that selects which of several similar objects is meant, exactly as it arrives - spatial and ordinal words ("left", "right", "middle", "back", "front", "top", "bottom", "upper", "lower"), count words ("both", plural forms), and the locating phrase as a whole ("next to the ramekin", "on the wooden cabinet", "in the top drawer"). Do not delete one, do not shorten the phrase, do not swap a selector for a synonym ("top" to "upper", "back" to "rear", "middle" to "centre"), and never replace a noun phrase with a pronoun. Rule 3 may still replace a class word inside such a phrase and rule 11 may still simplify its preposition; nothing else in it changes.
9. Keep the action verb exactly as the instruction gives it, and never substitute a verb implying a different motion or path from the one it uses ("slide", "drag", "shove", "sweep", "toss", "lift", "push" in place of whatever was written). Verb changes are not safe in either direction: the same swap is free on most tasks, worth +94 points on one and -60 on another, and nothing in the instruction text tells you which case you are in, so do not gamble.
10. Never add a directional particle to a verb or a preposition: do not turn "on" into "up onto", "to" into "over to", or "in" into "down into". Added particles have never gained on this policy and one cost 34 points.
11. Simplify an ornate spatial preposition the instruction already contains, checking the longer forms first so that the two-word forms are matched before the one-word forms: rewrite "up onto" to "on top of", "atop" to "on top of", "onto" to "on", and "into" to "in". Leave every other preposition exactly as it arrives, including "on", "in", "on top of", "next to", "beside", "in front of", "inside" and "to the front of". This preposition rewrite is the only edit you may make in addition to a noun replacement licensed by rule 3 or rule 5.
12. Leave capitalisation and terminal punctuation exactly as they arrive: do not capitalise a lowercase instruction, do not add or remove a full stop, and never treat typography as a fix.
13. Leave the sentence frame, the determiner and the politeness level as they arrive. Bare imperatives, requests ("can you ...", "could you ..."), statements of intent ("i want X on Y", "the X goes on Y", "the X should be put on Y") and filler ("just", "go ahead and") all perform the same, so neither add nor strip them, and do not change "the" to "that" or "that" to "the".
14. Make no edit whose purpose is length. Do not pad the instruction with extra description, and do not trim words out of a compound object name ("alphabet soup" to "soup", "cream cheese box" to "cream cheese"): both directions were measured flat, so neither buys anything, while trimming risks landing on a class word (rule 2) or removing a selector (rule 8).
15. Preserve every step of a multi-step instruction, in its original order, with its original pronoun references ("... and close it"). Never merge two steps into one, drop one, or reorder them; do not spell a pronoun back out as a noun; and do not split a single-step instruction into two steps either.

Rule 5 - odd fixture term to "stove".

Every listed term ranks at or below "stove" under the same verb, and the tail is catastrophic. The two other tasks that test the substitution do so on a destination rather than a switch-on target, and there it is noise in both directions: libero_90/19 "on the hot plate" 92 vs "on the stove" 94; libero_90/38 "on the hot plate" 40 vs "on the stove" 36 (the rewrite is 4 points worse). Those two rows are why the rule is scoped to the switched-on fixture and explicitly excludes destinations: outside the on/off sense there is no measured gain, and rule 1's default applies. Auditor objections accepted and fixed: (i) "burner" has been removed as a permitted output and moved to the trigger list, because with the verb held constant burner is far below stove - "switch on the burner" 64 vs "switch on the stove" 100 (-36), "start the burner" 40 vs "start the stove" 100 (-60), "start the burners" 48 (-52) - and is only equal at 100 under "turn on"; (ii) the "for one ring" clause is deleted, since one-ring-versus-whole-fixture is not decidable from instruction text; (iii) the trigger list is now explicitly closed, with "ring" and "induction plate" added and "stovetop" named as a term never to rewrite ("switch on the stovetop" scores 100); (iv) the movable-object test is replaced by the syntactic on/off test, which an applier can run without knowing whether a fixture is built in.

Note that "wooden" itself is harmless on libero_90/22 and /24 and costs 26 points on libero_goal/9, so the word is not the hazard - the state of the noun under it is. Colour swaps do nothing across five tasks: libero_90/10 black 94 / grey 96; libero_90/9 black 98 / grey 90 / dark 88; libero_90/30 black 76 / grey 72 / dark 72; libero_spatial/5 black 96 / dark 98; libero_90/25 black 0 / grey 0. Rule 7 - at most one substitution per referring phrase.
Five tasks show the same ladder, each single edit survivable and the stack not: libero_object/4 "the ketchup" 98 -> "the bottle" 96 -> "the red bottle" 58; libero_goal/9 "on the rack" 94 -> "on the stand" 76 -> "on the wooden stand" 50; libero_goal/8 "on the plate" 100 -> "on the dish" 48 -> "on the flat dish" 36; libero_goal/5 siblings "push the plate ..." 98 -> "slide that plate over ..." 62 -> "slide that dish over ..." 2 -> "slide that flat dish over ..." 0; libero_goal/7 verb swaps on the untouched noun are free ("turn on the stove" 100, "switch on the stove" 100, "start the stove" 100) while the same swaps on a substituted noun collapse ("turn on the burner" 100, "switch on the burner" 64, "start the burner" 40; "switch on the hot plate" 2, "power on the hot plate" 0). Rule 8 - keep the selectors.
Selector words are the only thing distinguishing several tasks that share a scene: libero_10/5 "back compartment" vs libero_90/78 and /81 "front compartment"; libero_90/22 "bottom drawer" vs libero_90/28 "top drawer" (and libero_90/23 targets both on one cabinet); libero_90/34 "the yellow and white mug" vs "the white mug" inside one sentence; libero_90/38 "the right moka pot" where libero_10/8 shows two pots on that stove; libero_90/17 "the middle black bowl" and "the back black bowl". Renaming a selector is free but never a gain, which is why the rule is "keep" and not "normalise": libero_90/22 "lower drawer" 100 vs "bottom drawer" 96; libero_90/28 "upper drawer" 38 vs "top drawer" 38; libero_spatial/4 "upper drawer" 90 vs "top drawer" 90; libero_90/17 "centre" 0 and "rear" 0 vs canonical 0; libero_10/5 "back slot" 100 vs "back compartment" 98. Auditor objections accepted and the rule reworded accordingly. First, no row anywhere in the file deletes or shortens a selector, so the deletion half is untested; it now rests on rule 1's emit-unchanged default plus the referent argument above, and is no longer presented as measured. Second, the one row that deletes a count word runs the other way and is free - libero_10/8 canonical "put both moka pots on the stove" 62 vs "put the moka pots on the stove" 66 - so no penalty is claimed for "both"; it is kept because dropping it changes how many objects are asked for, with no measured gain to buy that risk. Third, the previous draft's claim that "every loss above 18 points in this file is an edit that changed which object the phrase picks out" is false and has been deleted: libero_90/10 "up onto" 60 vs 94 is a preposition-only loss of 34, libero_goal/7 "start the burner" 40 vs "turn on the burner" 100 is a verb-only loss of 60, and libero_90/38 "lift" 34 vs "pick up" 54 is a verb-only loss of 20. Fourth, the rule now carries the same carve-out rule 4 has, so that rule 3's landmark substitution (libero_spatial/1 "next to the dish" 62 -> "next to the ramekin" 100) and rule 11's preposition rewrite are not forbidden by it; the previous draft had rules 3 and 8, and rules 8 and 11, giving opposite orders on the same input.
