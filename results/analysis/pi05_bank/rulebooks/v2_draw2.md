===RULES===
1. Inputs and referent resolution. You receive the incoming instruction and a scene description: a list of the objects and fixtures present, each under the name the robot was trained on (for example "black bowl", "plate", "ramekin", "ketchup", "tomato sauce", "moka pot", "stove", "cabinet"), possibly followed by extra descriptors (colour, material, size, shape, packaging). Rules 2, 3 and 11 replace a word in the instruction with a scene name, and each of them resolves the referent by this procedure and no other: (a) list the candidate scene objects the word could denote (each rule says how its candidates are found); (b) drop every candidate whose scene name already appears elsewhere in the sentence in a different role; (c) if the word carries colour, material, size or shape adjectives, keep only the candidates those adjectives fit, judged by the scene's descriptors where it gives them and otherwise by ordinary meaning ("flat" fits a plate and not a bowl, "small" fits a ramekin and not a plate, "red" fits the object the scene calls red); (d) if all remaining candidates share exactly one scene name, replace the word together with its colour/material/size/shape adjectives by that scene name, copied exactly as the scene lists it and without the scene's extra descriptors, keeping every position word, count word and locating phrase that was attached ("the red bottle on the left" becomes "the ketchup on the left"); if no candidate remains, or candidates with different names remain, leave the whole noun phrase exactly as written. Never resolve a word modified by "other", "another", "second" or "same". If no scene description is given, or it lists no objects, rules 3 and 11 do not fire at all and rule 2 fires only in its scene-free form.
2. Vague place words. When "dish" (with or without adjectives: "flat dish", "small dish") or "stand" ("wooden stand"), or by analogy "platter" or "holder", names the destination of a placement ("on the dish"), a landmark inside a locating phrase ("next to the dish", "on the dish" before "and place it"), or the object being moved ("slide that flat dish"), resolve it by rule 1 and replace it with the scene name. Candidates: for "dish"/"platter" in a destination or landmark slot, every plate, platter, saucer, ramekin or tray in the scene, and bowls only if the scene has none of those; for "dish" as the moved object, every plate-like object and every bowl; for "stand"/"holder", every rack, stand or holder. Examples: "put the bowl on the flat dish" becomes "put the bowl on the plate"; "put the wine bottle on the wooden stand" becomes "put the wine bottle on the rack"; "pick up the black bowl next to the dish and place it on the plate" becomes "... next to the ramekin ..." because "plate" is already used in the sentence; "put the dish on the plate" with one bowl in the scene becomes "put the bowl on the plate"; a bare "dish" that could be either of two differently named objects stays as written. Scene-free form (no scene description): only when "dish" is the destination directly after the moved object ("put/place/set X on the dish") and the word "plate" appears nowhere in the sentence, rewrite it to "on the plate"; in every other position leave "dish" and "stand" unchanged.
3. Class words for the object to be picked up. When the object to be picked up or moved is named by a packaging word ("bottle", "can", "tin", "jar", "carton", "box", "cup", "packet") or a category word ("drink", "snack", "food", "condiment", "item", "thing", or "sauce" when the scene lists no product whose own name ends in "sauce"), with no product word attached, resolve it by rule 1. Candidates: for a category word, every scene product the word ordinarily covers; for a packaging word, the scene products whose scene descriptors state that packaging, or, if the scene description states no packaging, every food or drink product in the scene (never guess a product's packaging from its name). Replace only when one scene name remains ("pick up the red bottle" with a scene listing ketchup (red), milk and orange juice becomes "pick up the ketchup"; "pick up the cup" with a scene listing one chocolate pudding cup and no mug becomes "pick up the chocolate pudding"); otherwise leave the noun phrase, adjectives included, exactly as written. A mug counts as a candidate for "cup", but when the only candidate is a mug leave "cup" as written (cup and mug are interchangeable, rule 10). A packaging word with a product word attached ("wine bottle", "cream cheese box", "pudding cup") is a specific name: never touch it (rule 4).
4. Never generalise, rename or shorten a specific name. Never replace a specific object or place name with a broader class, packaging, material or category word ("ketchup" must not become "sauce" or "bottle", "orange juice" must not become "drink" or "carton", "chocolate pudding" must not become "cup", "plate" must not become "dish", "rack" must not become "stand", "ramekin" must not become "dish"); never swap one specific name for another specific name ("moka pot" to "coffee pot" or back, "wine bottle" to "wine jug" or back); never shorten a compound name at all ("tomato sauce", "alphabet soup", "salad dressing", "orange juice" stay whole); never expand one either (rule 14). Unusual or rare names that arrive in the instruction (ramekin, rack, moka pot, caddy, tray, cream cheese, butter) are kept verbatim.
5. Cooking appliance words. Replace "hot plate", "hotplate", "burner", "burners", "stove burner", "hob", "cooktop", "stovetop", "cooker", "range", "griddle", "heating element", "ring", "induction plate", together with any colour, material or type adjective attached to it ("electric hot plate"), by "stove", whether the appliance is being switched on or is the surface something is put on ("switch on the electric hot plate" becomes "switch on the stove"; "put the moka pot on the burner" becomes "put the moka pot on the stove"). Apply this to those words unconditionally, whatever name the scene description uses for the appliance; it never applies to "microwave", "oven", "toaster", "kettle" or any other named appliance. Adjectives attached to "stove" itself stay ("electric stove" stays "electric stove"). If the appliance word carries a position selector ("the left burner", "the front burner"), leave the whole phrase as written, and rule 6 then does not fire either.
6. Switching the stove on. When the instruction's action is to switch the stove on and, after rule 5, the object is "the stove" (with whatever adjectives it already had), write the verb as "switch on": "turn on", "power on", "fire up", "ignite", "start", "activate", "light" all become "switch on" ("turn on the stove and put the moka pot on it" becomes "switch on the stove and put the moka pot on it"; "turn on the electric stove" becomes "switch on the electric stove"). A knob or dial motion with a stated activation purpose ("turn the black knob to start the burner", "twist the dial to light the stove") is one activation step written as two: rewrite the whole of it to "switch on the stove" (this is the one collapse rule 16 permits); a knob motion with no stated purpose is left as written. Do not fire when the object is any word other than "stove" ("turn on the left burner" stays as it is). This rule never applies to switching off; rule 8 handles those verbs.
7. Prepositions. Check the two-word forms first. "up onto", "atop", "up on top of", "on the top of" become "on top of" when the destination is a fixture with a top surface (cabinet, cupboard, shelf, counter, table, drawer unit, microwave) and "on" when the destination is a movable object or appliance surface (plate, rack, stove, bowl, tray): "place the black bowl up onto the cabinet" becomes "place the black bowl on top of the cabinet", "put the bowl up onto the plate" becomes "put the bowl on the plate"; "onto" becomes "on"; "into" becomes "in". Leave "inside", "next to", "beside", "in front of", "to the front of", "to the right of" and "on the right of" exactly as they arrive. Never delete or change the particle of a phrasal verb: "pick up", "turn on", "switch on", "power on", "fire up", "turn off", "switch off", "power off", "shut off", "push in" keep their particle; rules 6 and 8 decide what happens to those verbs.
8. Verb vocabulary. First map the off-verbs: "switch off", "power off", "shut off", "kill" applied to the stove become "turn off". Then: "grab", "grasp", "lift", "take hold of" become "pick up" (leave "hold" and "take" as written); "shut" used as a plain verb on a drawer, door, lid or microwave, "push in", "push ... in" ("push the top drawer in"), "push closed", "slide closed", "slide shut" become "close" ("... and shut it" becomes "... and close it"; "push in the open top drawer" becomes "close the open top drawer"); "slide", "scoot", "shove", "nudge", with or without a following "over", become "push" only when the destination is a spot on the same surface ("to the front/back/left/right of Y", "toward Y", "closer to Y", "up to Y", "against Y"), and the stranded "over" is deleted ("slide that plate over to the front of the stove" becomes "push the plate to the front of the stove", with "that" to "the" by rule 15); when the destination is "in/into/on/onto/on top of Y" leave the verb as written. Never map "move" (it may mean lift-and-place), and never map "stack", "open", "close", "push", "put", "place", "set" or "pick up" to anything else.
9. Treat "put", "place" and "set" as interchangeable placement verbs: leave whichever one arrives. When a verb must be supplied because the instruction had none (rule 15), use "put".
10. Leave everyday same-level synonyms exactly as they arrive: cup/mug, cabinet/cupboard, compartment/slot, top/upper, bottom/lower, middle/centre, back/rear, wood/wooden, moka pot/coffee pot/espresso pot, bottle/jug when a product word is attached ("wine jug"). These swaps average zero in both directions, so never spend an edit on them and never "correct" one to the scene's word.
11. Colour near-synonyms. When an object's colour word is a near-synonym of the colour in its scene name ("grey", "dark", "charcoal", "dark-coloured" for "black"; "cream", "off-white", "pale" for "white"), resolve the object by rule 1 with candidates = the scene objects of that kind (bowl, mug, ...) whose scene colour the word approximates; if exactly one scene name remains, use the scene's colour word ("put the grey bowl on the plate" becomes "put the black bowl on the plate"); otherwise leave it. Never change a colour word that already matches a scene name exactly, and never add or remove a colour word (rule 12).
12. Adjectives. Never add a colour, material, shape, size or state adjective the instruction does not carry, and never delete one that is part of the scene's name for the object ("black bowl", "white mug", "yellow and white mug", "wooden cabinet") or that picks one object out of several of the same kind. The only adjective deletions permitted are those bundled into rules 2, 3 and 5, where the adjective was attached to a class or appliance word that is being replaced by a specific name; an adjective attached to a word those rules leave unreplaced stays too ("put the wine bottle on the wooden stand" with no scene description stays as written).
13. Selectors and locating phrases. Keep every selector exactly as it arrives: position words ("left", "right", "middle", "centre", "back", "rear", "front", "top", "upper", "bottom", "lower"), count words ("both", "all", "two", plural endings), and the structure of every locating phrase ("next to the ...", "on the ...", "in the top drawer of the ..."). Do not add a selector, remove one, swap one for a synonym, or replace a noun phrase with a pronoun. Rules 2, 3, 5, 7 and 11 may replace a single noun, its colour/material adjective, or a preposition inside a locating phrase ("next to the dish" to "next to the ramekin", "on the hot plate" to "on the stove", "into the top drawer" to "in the top drawer"), but never the selector word and never the phrase's structure.
14. When a compound product name arrives shortened to its own head noun ("soup" for alphabet soup, "dressing" for salad dressing, "juice" for orange juice, "sauce" for tomato sauce, "cream cheese" for cream cheese box) and the scene lists exactly one product whose name ends in that word, leave it as written: expanding it back gains nothing. If the scene lists no product or several products ending in that word, the word is a category word: treat it under rule 3 (replace only if rule 1 leaves one scene name, otherwise leave it).
15. Sentence frame. Normalise to a lowercase bare imperative at zero expected gain: remove politeness and filler prefixes ("can you", "could you", "would you", "please", "just", "go ahead and"); remove the intensifier "right" only when it stands directly before a preposition ("right up to" becomes "to"), never the position word "right" ("the right plate", "to the right of" stay); lowercase the first letter; drop a terminal full stop, exclamation mark or question mark; change "that"/"this" to "the" only when it is the determiner directly before a noun phrase ("that plate" becomes "the plate"), never the relative pronoun ("the bowl that is on the plate" stays). Unwrap a statement of intent ("i want X on Y", "X goes on Y", "X should be put on Y", "X belongs in Y") into "put X on/in Y" only when the implied action is a placement with an explicit destination; if the implied action is anything else (switching on, opening, closing, pushing) leave the frame as it is. This rule never justifies accepting any other change.
16. Structure. Keep every step of a multi-step instruction in its original order with its original pronouns ("... and close it"); never merge two steps into one, never split one into two, never collapse "pick up X and place it on Y" into "put X on Y", and never expand "put X on Y" into the two-clause form. The single exception is rule 6's knob-motion-plus-purpose collapse, which is one activation action written as two clauses.
17. Precedence and default. Apply the rules in numerical order, 2 to 8 first, then 9 to 16. Each noun is resolved at most once, by the first rule that applies to it, and a noun one rule has replaced is not touched again by another; a locating phrase attached to a noun ("on the dish" in "the dark bowl on the dish") is resolved separately from the noun it modifies, so "dark" to "black" (rule 11) and "dish" to "ramekin" (rule 2) may both fire in one sentence. Every scene-dependent substitution (rules 2, 3, 11) fires only when rule 1 leaves exactly one scene name; when no rule applies with confidence, emit the instruction unchanged. Rules 2, 3, 5 and 6 carry the large expected gains; rules 7, 8 and 11 are small and never lost; everything else is hygiene or protection. A rewrite must never change which object or which place the instruction refers to: a wrong referent costs the whole task and no wording gain is worth it.
===RATIONALE===
All numbers are success % over n=50 rollouts on identical initial states; differences under ~18 points are noise. Deltas are "incoming row -> rewritten sibling" on the same task. libero_goal/5 and libero_90/82 have no canonical row, so their comparisons are sibling-to-sibling. Noise policy, applied the same way to every rule: a single-task delta of +18 is licensed only when the rewrite moves toward the trained template and fires on a rare input class ("push in" -> "close", +18 on libero_90/28, one of two "push in" rows in the file); it is not licensed when the rewrite moves away from a trained string on the majority of inputs (expanding "put X on Y" into "pick up X and place it on Y", +18 on libero_90/38, which would rewrite every single-clause canonical sitting at 94-100 with no measurement of that direction). That is why rule 8 keeps its +18 and rule 16 forgoes one of the same size.
